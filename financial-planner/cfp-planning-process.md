# CFP Board Financial Planning Process

Comprehensive guide to the 7-step financial planning process as defined by CFP Board Practice Standards.

## Overview

The CFP Board Practice Standards for the Financial Planning Process establish the professional requirements for delivering financial planning services. This process applies whether providing comprehensive financial planning or addressing a specific financial planning subject.

**Source:** CFP Board Code of Ethics and Standards of Conduct (effective June 30, 2020)

## The 7 Steps

### Step 1: Understanding the Client's Personal and Financial Circumstances

**Objective:** Obtain qualitative and quantitative information to understand client's situation

#### Information to Gather

**Qualitative Information:**
- Personal values and attitudes
- Financial goals and objectives
- Time horizons for goals
- Risk tolerance and capacity
- Life priorities and concerns
- Family situation and dynamics
- Behavioral biases and preferences
- Special needs or circumstances

**Quantitative Information:**
- Income (all sources)
- Expenses and cash flow
- Assets (liquid, invested, real estate, business interests)
- Liabilities (mortgages, loans, credit cards)
- Employee benefits
- Insurance coverage (life, disability, health, property/casualty, LTC, umbrella)
- Tax situation (current and deferred)
- Estate documents (wills, trusts, powers of attorney)
- Retirement accounts and pensions
- Education savings

#### Methods of Gathering Information

1. **Client questionnaires** - Standardized forms covering financial data
2. **Interview/meetings** - Discussion to understand values, goals, concerns
3. **Document review** - Tax returns, statements, insurance policies, estate documents
4. **Ongoing updates** - Regular reviews to capture changes

#### Practice Standard

**A CFP® professional must:**
- Obtain information sufficient to fulfill scope of engagement
- Analyze information to assess client's situation
- Address incomplete or inaccurate information with client

**Example:**
> During initial meeting, you learn:
> - Client age 45, married, two children (ages 12, 15)
> - Annual household income: $250,000
> - Retirement goal: Age 65 (20 years away)
> - College funding needed for both children in 3-6 years
> - Current 401(k): $400,000, IRA: $100,000
> - Life insurance: $250,000 group policy only
> - No estate documents beyond basic will from 10 years ago
>
> **Action:** Request tax returns, 401(k) statements, current budget/expenses, insurance policies, and schedule follow-up to discuss risk tolerance and specific goal priorities.

### Step 2: Identifying and Selecting Goals

**Objective:** Determine what the client wants to accomplish through financial planning

#### Goal Characteristics

Effective financial planning goals are **SMART:**
- **S**pecific - Clearly defined
- **M**easurable - Quantifiable target
- **A**chievable - Realistic given circumstances
- **R**elevant - Aligned with client values
- **T**ime-bound - Specific timeframe

#### Categories of Goals

**Short-Term (0-3 years):**
- Emergency fund establishment
- Debt reduction
- Annual vacation funding
- Home improvements

**Medium-Term (3-10 years):**
- College funding for children
- Home purchase or move-up
- Career change or business start
- Major purchases

**Long-Term (10+ years):**
- Retirement income sufficiency
- Financial independence
- Estate legacy planning
- Charitable giving objectives

#### Prioritization Process

When goals conflict due to resource constraints:

1. **Categorize** - Essential vs discretionary
2. **Sequence** - Time-sensitive vs flexible timing
3. **Tradeoffs** - Discuss implications of choosing one goal over another
4. **Client decision** - Client makes final priority decisions

**Example Prioritization Discussion:**
> **Competing Goals:**
> - Fund college education for two children: ~$200,000 needed in 3-6 years
> - Maximize retirement savings: Currently saving 10% ($25,000/year)
> - Purchase vacation home: $500,000 property
>
> **Analysis:**
> Current savings rate cannot fund all three simultaneously.
>
> **CFP® Professional Guidance:**
> "Let's prioritize: College is time-sensitive with fixed deadlines. Retirement is essential but has 20-year horizon. Vacation home is discretionary. I recommend prioritizing college funding and retirement savings now, reconsidering vacation home in 5-7 years after college expenses complete."
>
> **Client Decision:** Agrees to prioritize college and retirement, defer vacation home.

#### Practice Standard

**A CFP® professional must:**
- Identify potential goals with client
- Select and prioritize goals based on client values and objectives
- Discuss realistic expectations for goal achievement
- Obtain client agreement on selected goals

### Step 3: Analyzing the Client's Current Course of Action and Potential Alternative Courses of Action

**Objective:** Assess whether client's current approach will achieve goals; identify alternatives

#### Analysis Components

**Current Course Analysis:**
- Will current approach achieve stated goals?
- What are the projected outcomes (quantitative)?
- What risks exist in current approach?
- What opportunities are being missed?

**Alternative Courses:**
- Identify multiple strategies to achieve goals
- Compare advantages and disadvantages
- Assess feasibility and suitability
- Consider client preferences and constraints

#### Analytical Tools and Techniques

**Cash Flow Analysis:**
- Income vs expenses
- Surplus/deficit identification
- Opportunity for increased savings

**Net Worth Statement:**
- Assets minus liabilities
- Tracking growth over time
- Debt-to-asset ratio assessment

**Retirement Projection:**
- Monte Carlo simulation (probability of success)
- Straight-line projections with assumptions
- Gap analysis (needed vs projected)

**Tax Analysis:**
- Current effective tax rate
- Marginal tax bracket planning
- Tax-advantaged opportunities (401k, HSA, Roth)

**Insurance Needs Analysis:**
- Human capital approach (income replacement)
- Needs-based approach (debt, expenses, goals)
- Coverage gap identification

**Education Funding Projection:**
- Future cost estimates (inflation-adjusted)
- Current savings growth projection
- Funding gap and required monthly savings

**Estate Analysis:**
- Gross estate valuation
- Estate tax exposure (if applicable)
- Beneficiary designation review
- Titling and ownership structure

#### Example Analysis

**Scenario: Retirement Planning Analysis**

**Client Goal:** Retire at age 65 with $100,000/year income (in today's dollars)

**Current Course:**
- Age 45, 20 years to retirement
- Current retirement savings: $500,000
- Current contributions: $25,000/year (10% of income)
- Assumed return: 7% annually
- Inflation: 3%

**Projection:**
- At age 65: Approximately $2,100,000
- Using 4% withdrawal rule: $84,000/year
- Inflation-adjusted to today's dollars: ~$46,000/year
- **Gap:** $54,000/year shortfall

**Alternative Courses:**

**Alternative 1: Increase Savings**
- Increase contributions to $40,000/year (16% of income)
- Projected at age 65: $2,600,000
- 4% withdrawal: $104,000/year
- **Result:** Achieves goal

**Alternative 2: Delay Retirement**
- Retire at age 67 (2 extra years)
- Continue current $25,000/year contributions
- Projected at age 67: $2,500,000
- 4% withdrawal: $100,000/year
- **Result:** Achieves goal

**Alternative 3: Combination**
- Increase contributions to $32,000/year
- Retire at age 66 (1 extra year)
- Projected at age 66: $2,650,000
- 4% withdrawal: $106,000/year
- **Result:** Exceeds goal with moderate changes

**Analysis Presentation:**
"Based on our analysis, your current trajectory falls short of your retirement goal by approximately $54,000/year. I've identified three alternative approaches that would achieve your goal. Alternative 1 requires significant increase in savings now. Alternative 2 requires no change in savings but delays retirement. Alternative 3 provides a balanced approach with moderate increase in savings and one additional working year. Which approach aligns best with your priorities?"

#### Practice Standard

**A CFP® professional must:**
- Analyze client's current course of action
- Consider multiple alternative courses of action
- Evaluate how well each alternative meets goals
- Apply professional judgment based on client circumstances

### Step 4: Developing the Financial Planning Recommendation(s)

**Objective:** Create specific, actionable recommendations to help client achieve goals

#### Recommendation Requirements

**Each recommendation should:**
1. **Address specific goal** - Clearly tied to client objective
2. **Be actionable** - Specific steps client can take
3. **Be suitable** - Appropriate for client circumstances
4. **Consider tradeoffs** - Acknowledge costs and benefits
5. **Prioritize** - Sequence based on urgency and importance

#### Recommendation Framework

**Format:**
- **Goal addressed:** Which client objective does this serve?
- **Recommendation:** Specific action to take
- **Rationale:** Why this recommendation is suitable
- **Implementation:** How to execute
- **Timing:** When to implement
- **Priority:** Relative importance (High/Medium/Low)

#### Example Recommendations

**Example 1: Emergency Fund**

**Goal Addressed:** Financial security and protection from unexpected expenses

**Recommendation:** Establish emergency fund equal to 6 months of expenses ($60,000)

**Rationale:**
- Currently have only $10,000 in savings
- Dual-income household reduces risk, but 6 months provides adequate cushion
- Prevents need to liquidate investments or incur debt in emergency
- Provides peace of mind consistent with stated risk aversion

**Implementation:**
- Open high-yield savings account (Marcus, Ally, or similar - currently ~4.5% APY)
- Set up automatic transfer of $2,000/month from checking
- Reach full funding in 25 months
- Do not invest emergency fund in market (maintain liquidity and capital preservation)

**Timing:** Begin immediately

**Priority:** HIGH - Foundation for all other planning

---

**Example 2: Retirement Savings**

**Goal Addressed:** Retire at age 65 with $100,000/year income

**Recommendation:** Increase retirement contributions from $25,000 to $40,000 annually

**Breakdown:**
- Maximize 401(k): Increase from current $19,000 to $23,500 (2025 limit)
- Max employer match: Continue to receive full $7,500 employer match
- Add Roth IRA: Fund $7,000 annually ($583/month)
- Taxable brokerage: Contribute additional $2,000 annually if surplus exists

**Rationale:**
- Analysis shows $40,000/year contributions achieve retirement goal
- 401(k) provides immediate tax deduction (in 24% bracket = $5,880 tax savings)
- Roth IRA provides tax diversification for retirement
- Still maintains savings rate below 20% of gross income (sustainable)

**Implementation:**
- Contact HR to increase 401(k) election to 9.4% ($23,500 annual)
- Open Roth IRA at Vanguard/Fidelity/Schwab
- Set up automatic monthly contribution of $583 to Roth IRA
- Invest per recommended asset allocation (see Investment Policy Statement)

**Timing:** Implement before end of current year to maximize 2025 contributions

**Priority:** HIGH - Critical for retirement goal achievement

---

**Example 3: Life Insurance**

**Goal Addressed:** Family income protection in event of premature death

**Recommendation:** Purchase $1,500,000 20-year level term life insurance on primary earner

**Needs Analysis:**
- Income replacement: $150,000/year × 10 years = $1,500,000
- Less existing group coverage: -$250,000
- Less current retirement savings (would pass to spouse): -$500,000
- Mortgage balance: $400,000
- College funding need: $200,000
- Emergency fund/final expenses: $100,000
- **Total need:** $1,450,000 → Round to $1,500,000

**Rationale:**
- Current group coverage of $250,000 insufficient for family needs
- Term insurance provides most cost-effective pure death benefit protection
- 20-year term aligns with timeframe until children independent and mortgage paid
- Estimated cost: ~$80-100/month for healthy 45-year-old

**Implementation:**
- Obtain quotes from 3-4 highly-rated carriers (A.M. Best A+ or higher)
- Complete medical exam and underwriting
- Consider supplemental coverage on spouse ($500,000)
- Review group coverage for portability/conversion options

**Timing:** Within next 60 days (health can change)

**Priority:** HIGH - Protects against catastrophic family financial loss

---

**Example 4: Estate Planning**

**Goal Addressed:** Ensure assets pass efficiently to spouse and children; provide for minor children

**Recommendation:** Update estate documents to reflect current circumstances and goals

**Specific Documents:**
1. **Revocable Living Trust** - Avoid probate, provide management if incapacitated
2. **Pour-Over Will** - Backstop for any assets not titled to trust
3. **Durable Power of Attorney** - Financial decision-making if incapacitated
4. **Healthcare Power of Attorney** - Medical decision-making authority
5. **HIPAA Authorization** - Access to medical records
6. **Living Will/Advance Directive** - End-of-life care preferences

**Rationale:**
- Current will is 10 years old, pre-dates second child
- No trust in place (estate of ~$1.5M would go through probate)
- No powers of attorney (spouse cannot manage finances if incapacitated)
- Minor children need guardianship designation and trustee for inheritance

**Implementation:**
- Engage estate planning attorney (referral: Smith & Associates)
- Review and sign documents
- Re-title assets to trust (home, brokerage accounts)
- Update beneficiary designations on retirement accounts and life insurance
- Provide copies of POA to financial institutions

**Timing:** Complete within 6 months

**Priority:** MEDIUM - Important but not immediately urgent (healthy, age 45)

---

#### Integration of Recommendations

Recommendations should work together as an integrated plan:

**Example Integration:**
1. **Emergency fund** (HIGH) - Provides foundation, complete first
2. **Life insurance** (HIGH) - Protects family, implement concurrently
3. **Retirement contributions** (HIGH) - Begin after emergency fund funded
4. **College savings** (MEDIUM) - Start 529 contributions alongside retirement
5. **Estate planning** (MEDIUM) - Complete within 6 months
6. **Tax optimization** (ONGOING) - Review annually, harvest losses, Roth conversions
7. **Investment rebalancing** (ONGOING) - Quarterly review, annual rebalance

#### Practice Standard

**A CFP® professional must:**
- Develop recommendation(s) considering all relevant aspects of client's situation
- Base recommendations on analysis performed in Step 3
- Use professional judgment applying principles of financial planning
- Recommendations must be designed to maximize potential for meeting client goals

### Step 5: Presenting the Financial Planning Recommendation(s)

**Objective:** Communicate recommendations clearly and obtain client understanding and agreement

#### Presentation Requirements

**Content to Include:**
1. **Summary of client goals** - Restate what client wants to accomplish
2. **Key assumptions** - Return rates, inflation, tax rates, life expectancy
3. **Analysis results** - Current trajectory vs goals (from Step 3)
4. **Recommendations** - Specific actions with rationale (from Step 4)
5. **Expected outcomes** - Projected results if recommendations implemented
6. **Alternatives considered** - Options discussed and why recommendation was selected
7. **Risks and limitations** - What could go wrong, what is uncertain

#### Presentation Format

**Written Financial Plan Components:**

**Executive Summary (2-3 pages):**
- Client snapshot
- Key findings
- Primary recommendations
- Expected outcomes

**Current Situation (5-10 pages):**
- Net worth statement
- Cash flow analysis
- Current asset allocation
- Tax situation summary
- Insurance coverage review
- Estate planning status

**Goals and Objectives (2-3 pages):**
- Short-term goals
- Medium-term goals
- Long-term goals
- Priorities

**Analysis and Projections (10-15 pages):**
- Retirement projection (Monte Carlo simulation)
- Education funding analysis
- Tax planning opportunities
- Insurance needs analysis
- Estate tax projection (if applicable)

**Recommendations (15-20 pages):**
- Detailed recommendations by category
- Implementation guidance
- Priority sequencing
- Cost/benefit analysis

**Appendices:**
- Assumptions used
- Client-provided documents
- Product illustrations (if applicable)
- Resource references

**Oral Presentation (60-90 minutes):**
1. **Review goals** (5 min) - Confirm shared understanding
2. **Present current situation** (10 min) - Where you are today
3. **Show analysis** (15 min) - Where you're headed on current course
4. **Present recommendations** (30 min) - What to change and why
5. **Discuss implementation** (15 min) - Next steps and timeline
6. **Q&A** (15 min) - Address questions and concerns

#### Communication Best Practices

**Use Plain Language:**
- Avoid jargon and technical terms
- Explain concepts using analogies and examples
- Check for understanding

**Visual Aids:**
- Charts showing current vs recommended trajectory
- Pie charts for asset allocation
- Bar graphs for income replacement needs
- Timeline graphics for implementation sequence

**Customize to Client:**
- Level of detail appropriate to client sophistication
- Format suited to learning style (visual, analytical, big picture)
- Pace aligned with client comfort

**Encourage Questions:**
- Pause regularly for questions
- Invite clarification requests
- Address concerns thoroughly

#### Example Presentation Excerpt

**Retirement Planning Presentation:**

"Let me show you where you're headed on your current path versus what's needed to achieve your goal.

[**Chart 1: Retirement Projection - Current Course**]

Based on your current savings of $500,000 and contributions of $25,000 per year, with a 7% average return over the next 20 years, you'll accumulate approximately $2.1 million by age 65.

Using the sustainable withdrawal rate of 4%, that provides $84,000 per year. Adjusted for inflation over 20 years, that's about $46,000 in today's purchasing power.

Your goal is $100,000 per year in today's dollars, so there's a shortfall of $54,000 per year, or about 54% of your target.

[**Chart 2: Retirement Projection - Recommended Course**]

If we increase your annual contributions to $40,000 per year—that's an additional $15,000 annually—you'll accumulate $2.6 million by age 65.

That provides $104,000 per year at a 4% withdrawal rate, achieving your goal.

[**Chart 3: Cash Flow Impact**]

The additional $15,000 in retirement contributions equals $1,250 per month. However, because these are pre-tax 401(k) contributions, your take-home pay only decreases by about $950 per month (you save $300/month in taxes at your 24% marginal rate).

Looking at your current cash flow, you have monthly surplus of $1,500 after all expenses. Allocating $950 of that to retirement still leaves $550 monthly cushion.

Does this increase feel manageable given your current budget?"

[**Client response and discussion**]

"Great question about what happens if you receive inheritance from your parents as expected in 10-15 years. If that occurs, you could reduce contributions at that time or even retire earlier. But I recommend we plan conservatively assuming only resources within your control today. Any inheritance would be upside, allowing you to adjust the plan favorably when it happens."

#### Conflict of Interest Disclosure

During presentation, disclose:
- How you are compensated (fee-only, fee-based, commission)
- Any conflicts related to recommended products
- Material limitations on recommendations
- If you receive compensation from product sales or referrals

**Example Disclosure:**
"Before we proceed, I want to be transparent about how I'm compensated. I'm a fee-only advisor, meaning I'm paid solely by you through our planning fee agreement. I do not receive commissions or compensation from any product sales. When we implement recommendations requiring products like insurance or investments, I'll refer you to third-party providers. I do not receive referral fees or any compensation from those providers. My only financial interest is in providing advice that serves your best interest."

#### Practice Standard

**A CFP® professional must:**
- Present recommendation(s) to client
- Communicate basis and how they meet goals
- Provide client opportunity to ask questions and express concerns
- Address conflicts of interest
- Make and/or implement only recommendations client understands

### Step 6: Implementing the Financial Planning Recommendation(s)

**Objective:** Execute the agreed-upon recommendations

#### Scope of Implementation Services

Implementation services depend on engagement:

**Full Implementation (planner implements directly):**
- Opening accounts
- Purchasing insurance
- Executing trades
- Establishing automatic contributions
- Re-titling assets
- Coordinating with attorneys, CPAs, other professionals

**Limited Implementation (planner coordinates):**
- Provide referrals to product providers
- Review proposals and illustrations
- Coordinate with client's existing professionals
- Monitor progress and completion

**No Implementation (advice only):**
- Provide recommendations only
- Client responsible for all implementation
- May offer follow-up review service

#### Implementation Responsibilities

**CFP® Professional Responsibilities:**
- Identify actions needed to implement each recommendation
- Assign responsibilities (who does what)
- Establish timeline for implementation
- Coordinate with third parties as needed
- Document implementation activity

**Client Responsibilities:**
- Make decisions on recommendations
- Provide necessary information and documentation
- Take actions assigned to them
- Communicate with planner on progress
- Authorize transactions and changes

**Third-Party Responsibilities:**
- Insurance agents: Submit applications, complete underwriting
- Attorneys: Draft estate documents
- CPAs: Prepare tax returns, advise on tax implications
- Custodians: Open accounts, execute transactions

#### Implementation Timeline Example

**Month 1:**
- ✅ Open high-yield savings account
- ✅ Set up automatic transfer for emergency fund
- ✅ Increase 401(k) contribution election
- ✅ Submit term life insurance application

**Month 2:**
- ✅ Complete life insurance medical exam
- ✅ Open Roth IRA accounts (self and spouse)
- ✅ Set up automatic monthly Roth contributions
- ✅ Schedule meeting with estate planning attorney

**Month 3:**
- ✅ Life insurance policy issued
- ✅ First Roth IRA contributions made
- ✅ Initial estate planning meeting completed
- ⬜ Review estate document drafts

**Month 4:**
- ⬜ Sign estate documents
- ⬜ Re-title home to trust
- ⬜ Re-title brokerage accounts to trust
- ⬜ Update beneficiary designations

**Months 5-12:**
- ⬜ Continue emergency fund accumulation
- ⬜ Monitor retirement contributions
- ⬜ Quarterly investment rebalancing as needed
- ⬜ Annual review meeting

#### Monitoring Implementation

Track progress regularly:

**Implementation Checklist:**
- [ ] Emergency fund account opened
- [ ] Automatic transfers established
- [ ] 401(k) election increased (confirm via paystub)
- [ ] Employer match verification
- [ ] Roth IRA accounts opened
- [ ] Automatic Roth contributions set up
- [ ] Life insurance application submitted
- [ ] Medical exam completed
- [ ] Policy delivered and premiums set up
- [ ] Estate attorney engaged
- [ ] Documents drafted and reviewed
- [ ] Documents signed and notarized
- [ ] Assets re-titled to trust
- [ ] Beneficiary designations updated

**Communication with Client:**
- Weekly/bi-weekly check-ins during active implementation
- Email updates on completed items
- Prompt follow-up on items requiring client action
- Celebrate milestones (policy issued, documents signed, etc.)

#### Practice Standard

**A CFP® professional must:**
- Identify actions needed to implement recommendation(s)
- Determine division of responsibilities
- Assist client with implementation (if within scope)
- Coordinate with other professionals as needed
- Document implementation actions

### Step 7: Monitoring Progress and Updating

**Objective:** Monitor client's situation, track goal progress, update plan as circumstances change

#### Monitoring Frequency

**Ongoing (As Needed):**
- Major life events (marriage, birth, death, divorce)
- Job changes
- Inheritance or windfall
- Health changes
- Market volatility concerns

**Quarterly:**
- Investment performance review
- Rebalancing assessment
- Cash flow check-in
- Progress toward short-term goals

**Annual:**
- Comprehensive plan review
- Goal reassessment
- Tax planning for upcoming year
- Beneficiary designation verification
- Insurance coverage review
- Net worth and cash flow update

**Milestone-Based:**
- Child starting college
- Approaching retirement (5 years out)
- Retirement transition
- Age 73 (RMD planning)
- Age 65 (Medicare enrollment)

#### Annual Review Meeting Agenda

**Pre-Meeting Preparation:**
- Request updated documents (tax return, paystubs, account statements)
- Update net worth and cash flow
- Review progress vs last year's projections
- Prepare updated analysis and projections

**Meeting Agenda (90 minutes):**

1. **Life Updates** (15 min)
   - Changes in family, career, health
   - New goals or changed priorities
   - Concerns or questions

2. **Goal Progress Review** (20 min)
   - Emergency fund status
   - Retirement savings progress
   - Education funding accumulation
   - Other goal-specific tracking

3. **Investment Performance** (15 min)
   - Portfolio return vs benchmarks
   - Asset allocation vs target
   - Rebalancing recommendations
   - Tax-loss harvesting opportunities

4. **Plan Updates** (25 min)
   - Revised projections (retirement, education)
   - Recommended adjustments
   - New opportunities (Roth conversions, etc.)
   - Tax planning for next year

5. **Action Items** (10 min)
   - Implementation tasks from today's meeting
   - Timeline and responsibilities
   - Next review meeting scheduling

6. **Q&A** (5 min)

#### Triggering Events for Plan Updates

**Require Immediate Update:**
- Divorce or separation
- Death of spouse or family member
- Job loss or significant income change
- Disability or health crisis
- Inheritance or windfall
- Birth or adoption of child
- Starting a business

**Require Review (may or may not change plan):**
- Job change with similar income
- Home purchase or sale
- Market decline of 20%+
- Interest rate changes of 2%+
- Tax law changes
- Retirement plan changes at work

#### Practice Standard

**A CFP® professional must:**
- Define monitoring and updating responsibilities with client
- Monitor client's progress
- Obtain current information at appropriate intervals
- Update recommendations, goals, or implementation as circumstances change

## Engagement Letter Provisions

The engagement letter should specify:

**Scope of Engagement:**
- Is this comprehensive financial planning or modular (specific topics)?
- Which steps will be performed?
- What deliverables will be provided?

**Responsibilities:**
- What planner will do
- What client must do
- Division of implementation responsibilities

**Compensation:**
- How planner is paid (flat fee, hourly, AUM, commission)
- Amount and payment schedule
- Refund policy if applicable

**Duration:**
- Ongoing relationship or one-time plan?
- Frequency of reviews
- How relationship can be terminated

**Compliance:**
- Fiduciary acknowledgment
- Conflicts of interest disclosure
- Limitations on recommendations

## Documentation Requirements

**Required Documentation:**

1. **Engagement Letter** - Signed by client and planner
2. **Financial Planning Questionnaire** - Completed by client
3. **Privacy Notice** - Provided to client
4. **ADV Part 2A** - Delivered to client (if RIA)
5. **Form CRS** - Delivered to client
6. **Financial Plan** - Written plan document
7. **Meeting Notes** - Document all client meetings
8. **Implementation Records** - Account openings, transactions, etc.
9. **Annual Review Documents** - Updated plans and meeting notes

**Retention:**
SEC-registered RIAs must retain records for 5 years (first 2 years in accessible location)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Step 1 (Understanding):**
- ❌ Relying solely on client-provided information without verification
- ❌ Failing to understand client's values and motivations beyond numbers
- ❌ Not updating information regularly

**Step 2 (Goals):**
- ❌ Imposing your goals on client rather than eliciting theirs
- ❌ Failing to prioritize when goals conflict
- ❌ Setting unrealistic goals without discussing tradeoffs

**Step 3 (Analysis):**
- ❌ Using overly optimistic assumptions
- ❌ Failing to consider multiple alternatives
- ❌ Not stress-testing plan for adverse scenarios

**Step 4 (Recommendations):**
- ❌ Making recommendations that aren't suitable for client's risk tolerance
- ❌ Recommending products that benefit planner more than client
- ❌ Failing to integrate recommendations across planning areas

**Step 5 (Presentation):**
- ❌ Using jargon client doesn't understand
- ❌ Overwhelming client with too much detail
- ❌ Not adequately disclosing conflicts of interest

**Step 6 (Implementation):**
- ❌ Assuming client will implement on their own without follow-up
- ❌ Failing to coordinate with other professionals
- ❌ Not documenting implementation activity

**Step 7 (Monitoring):**
- ❌ Not establishing clear monitoring schedule
- ❌ Failing to update plan when circumstances change
- ❌ Losing contact with client after plan delivery
