# Underwriting Guide for Protection Insurance

## What is Underwriting?

**Definition**: Insurer's assessment of risk to determine:
- Whether to accept application
- What premium to charge
- Any exclusions or special terms

**Purpose**: Ensure fair pricing based on individual risk

## Underwriting Process

### 1. Application Form

**Standard Questions**:
- Personal details (age, occupation, income)
- Medical history (last 5 years typically)
- Family history (parents, siblings)
- Lifestyle (smoking, alcohol, drugs)
- Height and weight (BMI)
- Occupation and duties
- Hobbies and pastimes

**Key Principle**: **Utmost good faith** (uberrimae fidei)
- Must disclose all material facts
- Even if not asked specifically
- Failure = policy voidable

### 2. Medical Evidence

**Levels of Evidence**:

**No Medical Evidence Required** (Non-Medical Limits):
- Application form only
- Up to certain limits (vary by provider and age)
- Typical: £250k-£500k life cover (age under 45)

**GP Report**:
- Request medical records from GP
- Last 5-10 years
- Insurer reviews
- Common for larger sums or medical history

**Medical Examination**:
- Height, weight, blood pressure
- Urine sample (drugs, diabetes, kidney function)
- Blood test (cholesterol, liver, HIV, etc.)
- ECG (heart function) if high sum/older
- Conducted by insurer's medical examiner

**Specialist Reports**:
- Cardiologist (heart conditions)
- Oncologist (cancer history)
- Psychiatrist (mental health)
- Hospital records
- For specific conditions or concerns

### 3. Underwriting Decision

**Timeline**:
- Simple cases: 24-48 hours
- GP report needed: 2-4 weeks
- Medical exam needed: 2-3 weeks
- Specialist reports: 4-8 weeks
- Complex cases: 8-12 weeks

**Outcomes**:
1. Standard terms (accepted at normal rates)
2. Loading (increased premium)
3. Exclusion (specific condition excluded)
4. Postponement (await treatment/results)
5. Decline (uninsurable)

## Non-Medical Limits by Age and Provider

**Typical Limits** (life insurance):

| Age | Standard Limit | Better Providers |
|-----|----------------|------------------|
| 18-40 | £500,000 | £750,000 |
| 41-45 | £350,000 | £500,000 |
| 46-50 | £250,000 | £350,000 |
| 51-55 | £150,000 | £200,000 |
| 56+ | £100,000 | £150,000 |

**Critical Illness**: Usually lower (50-75% of life insurance limits)

**Income Protection**: Often lower limits, more medical evidence required

## Underwriting Outcomes

### 1. Standard Terms

**Definition**: Accepted at normal premium rates

**Means**:
- Average health for your age
- No material health issues
- No hazardous occupation/hobbies

**Example**:
- Age 35, non-smoker, office worker, good health
- £300,000 life insurance
- Premium: £15/month (standard rate)

### 2. Loading (Increased Premium)

**Definition**: Accepted but at higher premium

**Expressed As**:
- **Flat extra**: £X per £1,000 sum assured
- **% loading**: +25%, +50%, +100%, etc.
- **Age rating**: Treated as X years older

**Example 1: Flat Extra**
- Standard rate: £20/month
- Loading: +£2 per £1,000 sum assured
- Sum assured: £100,000
- Extra: £200/year (£16.67/month)
- Total: £36.67/month

**Example 2: % Loading**
- Standard rate: £30/month
- Loading: +50%
- Total: £45/month

**Example 3: Age Rating**
- Actual age: 40
- Age rated: +5 years (treated as 45)
- Premium: Rate for age 45 (e.g., £35 instead of £25)

**Duration**:
- Temporary: For 2-5 years (review after treatment)
- Permanent: For life of policy

**Common Loadings**:
- Smoker: +50-100%
- High BMI: +25-75%
- High cholesterol: +25-50%
- Diabetes: +50-150%
- Family history cancer: +25-50%

### 3. Exclusion

**Definition**: Condition excluded from cover

**How It Works**:
- Policy accepted
- Specific condition excluded
- Premium reduced
- All other conditions covered

**Example**:
- Asthma history
- Exclusion: "All claims relating to respiratory conditions excluded"
- Premium: Lower than with loading
- Heart attack, cancer, accident: Covered ✓
- Death from respiratory failure: Not covered ✗

**Pros**:
- Lower premium
- Other conditions still covered
- Better than decline

**Cons**:
- Gap in cover
- Excluded condition might be claim trigger
- Must disclose to future insurers

**When Offered**:
- Manageable condition
- Unlikely to be fatal
- Client wants reduced premium

**Common Exclusions**:
- Back/spinal conditions
- Mental health conditions
- Specific diagnosed condition

### 4. Postponement

**Definition**: Decision delayed pending further information

**Reasons**:
- Awaiting test results
- Recent diagnosis (need time to stabilize)
- Treatment ongoing
- Awaiting specialist opinion

**Timeline**:
- 6 months: Common
- 12 months: For more serious conditions
- 2+ years: Major conditions (cancer, heart attack)

**Example**:
- Recently diagnosed hypertension
- Postpone 6 months
- Await medication stabilization
- Reapply once controlled

**What to Do**:
- Complete treatment
- Follow medical advice
- Gather medical evidence
- Reapply when conditions met

### 5. Decline

**Definition**: Application refused

**Reasons**:
- Too high risk
- Uninsurable condition
- Very poor health
- Recent serious diagnosis
- Lifestyle factors (heavy drug use)

**Common Decline Reasons**:
- Recent cancer diagnosis
- Recent heart attack/stroke
- Serious ongoing medical condition
- Multiple health issues combined
- Very high BMI (50+)
- HIV positive (some insurers)
- Significant mental health history

**Options After Decline**:
- Try specialist provider
- Try different product (IP vs life)
- Guaranteed acceptance products (limited cover)
- Improve health and reapply
- Group insurance through employer

## Medical Conditions and Underwriting

### Cardiovascular

**High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)**:
- Controlled (medication): +25-50% loading
- Uncontrolled: Postpone or decline
- Under 140/90 with medication: Often standard or small loading

**High Cholesterol**:
- Controlled (statins): +0-25% loading
- Under 5.0 total cholesterol: Often standard
- Family history only (no diagnosis): Usually standard

**Heart Attack (Previous)**:
- Recent (<2 years): Decline or heavy loading
- 2-5 years ago: +100-200% loading
- 5+ years, no complications: +50-100% loading

**Angina**:
- Stable, controlled: +50-100% loading
- Unstable: Decline or postpone

### Cancer

**Cancer (Previous)**:
- **0-2 years**: Usually decline
- **2-5 years**: Heavy loading (+100-300%) or postpone
- **5+ years**: Moderate loading (+25-100%) depending on type
- **10+ years**: Small loading or standard (some cancers)

**Type Matters**:
- Skin cancer (basal cell): Often standard
- Testicular/thyroid (early stage, treated): +25-50%
- Breast/bowel (5+ years clear): +50-100%
- Pancreatic/lung: Very difficult or decline

**Critical Illness**:
- Previous cancer: Usually excluded (specific cancer)
- Or very heavy loading

### Diabetes

**Type 1 (Insulin Dependent)**:
- +50-150% loading (life insurance)
- Higher loading for CI
- IP: Pancreatic exclusion

**Type 2 (Non-Insulin)**:
- Controlled by diet: +25-50%
- Controlled by tablets: +50-75%
- Insulin required: +75-150%

**Factors**:
- HbA1c level (control)
- Complications (retinopathy, neuropathy)
- Age at diagnosis
- Time since diagnosis

### Mental Health

**Depression/Anxiety**:
- **Single episode, recovered >2 years**: Standard or small loading
- **Current treatment**: +25-75% loading
- **Multiple episodes**: +50-100% loading
- **Recent hospitalization**: Postpone or decline

**Serious Mental Health**:
- Bipolar disorder: +100-200% loading or decline
- Schizophrenia: Usually decline
- Severe depression: Heavy loading or decline

**Income Protection**:
- Mental health conditions: Often excluded
- Or maximum 12-24 month benefit period

### Musculoskeletal

**Back Pain**:
- Occasional, no time off work: Standard
- Chronic, regular treatment: Exclusion or loading
- Previous surgery: +25-50% or exclusion

**Joint Problems**:
- Minor: Standard
- Requiring surgery: Postpone until recovered
- Chronic conditions: Loading or exclusion

### Other Conditions

**Asthma**:
- Well controlled: Standard to +25%
- Regular hospital admissions: +50% or decline

**Epilepsy**:
- Well controlled (no fits 2+ years): +25-75%
- Recent fits: +100% or decline

**Kidney Disease**:
- Minor: +25-50%
- Chronic kidney disease: +100-200% or decline

**Liver Disease**:
- Alcohol-related: Heavy loading or decline
- Non-alcohol (controlled): +50-100%

## Lifestyle Factors

### Smoking

**Definition** (most insurers): Any tobacco in last 12 months
- Cigarettes: Smoker
- Cigars: Smoker
- Pipe: Smoker
- E-cigarettes/vaping: Smoker (most insurers)

**Impact**:
- Life insurance: +50-100% loading
- Critical illness: +75-125% loading
- Income protection: +30-50% loading

**Example**:
- Non-smoker life premium: £20/month
- Smoker life premium: £40/month
- **Double the cost**

**Quitting**:
- 12 months tobacco-free: Apply as non-smoker
- Must disclose previous smoking
- Some insurers test (cotinine test)

### Body Mass Index (BMI)

**Standard Range**: BMI 18-30 (approximately)

**Underwriting Impact**:

| BMI | Impact |
|-----|--------|
| <18 | Questions/investigation (underweight) |
| 18-25 | Standard |
| 25-30 | Standard (often) |
| 30-35 | +25-50% loading |
| 35-40 | +75-150% loading |
| 40+ | Heavy loading or decline |

**Calculation**: Weight (kg) / Height (m)²

**Example**:
- Height: 1.75m (5'9")
- Weight: 95kg (15 stone)
- BMI: 31 (overweight)
- Likely impact: +25-50% loading

### Alcohol

**Units Per Week**:
- <14 units: Standard
- 14-21 units: Standard or questions
- 21-30 units: +25-50% loading
- 30-50 units: +50-100% loading or decline
- >50 units: Usually decline

**1 Unit**:
- 1/2 pint normal strength beer
- Small glass (125ml) wine (12%)
- Single spirit (25ml)

**Recent Treatment**:
- Rehab/counseling: +100% or postpone
- Alcoholism diagnosis: Decline or heavy loading

### Drugs

**Recreational Drugs**:
- Occasional cannabis: +25-75% loading
- Regular cannabis: +75-150% loading
- Class A drugs (cocaine, heroin): Decline
- Previous drug use (clean >5 years): +0-50%

**Prescription Drugs**:
- Assessed on underlying condition
- Methadone (addiction treatment): Heavy loading or decline

## Occupation and Hobbies

### Occupation Classes

**Class 1** (Professional/Clerical):
- Office workers, managers, teachers
- Lowest premiums
- Standard rates

**Class 2** (Skilled Non-Manual):
- Shop managers, sales, technicians
- Slight loading (5-15%)

**Class 3** (Skilled Manual):
- Electricians, plumbers, mechanics
- Moderate loading (15-30%)

**Class 4** (Unskilled/Heavy Manual):
- Construction workers, factory workers
- Higher loading (30-50%)

**Hazardous Occupations** (Specialist Underwriting):
- Police, firefighters: +25-100%
- Offshore workers: +50-150%
- Military (active duty): +100-300% or decline
- Divers, pilots: Specialist pricing

### Hazardous Hobbies

**Low Risk** (Standard):
- Running, gym, cycling (casual)
- Swimming

**Medium Risk** (+25-75%):
- Scuba diving (recreational, <30m)
- Skiing (piste)
- Motorcycling (road)

**High Risk** (+100-300% or exclusion):
- Scuba diving (deep, wreck)
- Mountaineering (high altitude)
- Motor racing
- Aviation (private pilot)
- Skydiving

**Very High Risk** (Decline or specialist):
- Base jumping
- Wing suit flying
- Cave diving

## Family History

**First-Degree Relatives** (parents, siblings):
- Cancer under 60: +25-50% loading (CI)
- Heart disease under 60: +25-50% loading
- Multiple family history: Higher loadings

**Impact**:
- Life insurance: Moderate impact
- Critical illness: Significant impact
- Income protection: Minimal impact

**Example**:
- Father died of heart attack age 55
- Critical illness loading: +25-50%
- Life insurance loading: +0-25%

## Age and Gender

**Age**:
- Younger: Lower premiums
- Older: Higher premiums (exponential increase)

**Gender**:
- Life insurance: Men more expensive (die younger)
- Critical illness: Women more expensive (claim more)
- Income protection: Women more expensive

**Example (Life Insurance)**:
- Male age 40: £20/month
- Female age 40: £15/month
- (Same cover, same health)

## Improving Underwriting Terms

### Before Applying

**Health Improvements**:
- Lose weight (if overweight)
- Quit smoking (12 months before applying)
- Reduce alcohol intake
- Control blood pressure (medication)
- Exercise regularly (improves health markers)

**Medical Management**:
- Ensure conditions well controlled
- Have regular checkups
- Follow medical advice
- Keep medications up to date

**Timing**:
- After treatment completion
- When condition stable
- Not during health episode

### After Poor Terms

**Options**:
1. **Accept terms** (if reasonable)
2. **Improve health and reapply** (if postponed)
3. **Try different provider** (underwriting varies)
4. **Specialist broker** (access to more insurers)
5. **Different product** (IP instead of CI, or vice versa)
6. **Guaranteed acceptance products** (limited cover, older ages)

### Challenging Decisions

**When to Challenge**:
- Clearly incorrect information
- Condition better than underwriter assessed
- New medical evidence available
- Comparable case accepted elsewhere

**How to Challenge**:
- Provide additional medical evidence
- GP letter explaining condition
- Specialist report
- Request review
- Escalate to chief medical officer

**When Not to Challenge**:
- Decision is fair based on evidence
- Standard underwriting for your condition
- All providers would decide similarly

## Disclosure and Non-Disclosure

### What Must Be Disclosed

**All Material Facts**:
- All medical conditions (even if resolved)
- All consultations (last 5 years)
- All medications (current and recent)
- All investigations/tests
- All family history (first-degree relatives)

**"Material Fact"**: Anything that would influence underwriting decision

### Non-Disclosure Consequences

**Policy Voidable**:
- Insurer can refuse claim
- Insurer can cancel policy
- Premiums not refunded

**Example**:
- Didn't disclose high blood pressure
- Died from stroke
- Insurer investigates, finds non-disclosure
- Claim declined, no payout

**Criminal Fraud**:
- Deliberate non-disclosure
- Can result in prosecution

**Always Disclose**: Even if think it's minor

### Common Non-Disclosure Issues

**"I Forgot"**: Not an excuse
**"GP Didn't Diagnose It"**: Still must disclose symptoms
**"It Was Only Minor"**: Insurer decides if material
**"It Was Ages Ago"**: Still disclose if within asked period

## Provider Differences

**Liberal Underwriting**:
- More likely to accept at standard rates
- Lower loadings
- More flexible
- May have higher base premiums

**Strict Underwriting**:
- More likely to load or decline
- Higher loadings for same condition
- Less flexible
- May have lower base premiums (for healthy lives)

**Specialist Providers**:
- Specific conditions (e.g., diabetes specialists)
- Older ages
- Guaranteed acceptance products
- Impaired lives

## Best Practice

**For Advisers**:
- Gather full medical history before applying
- Pre-assess likely underwriting outcome
- Apply to most suitable provider
- Prepare client for possible outcomes
- Have backup options ready
- Don't apply to multiple providers simultaneously (ratings recorded)

**For Clients**:
- Disclose everything
- Be honest on application
- Improve health before applying if possible
- Understand that terms may not be standard
- Don't give up if first application declined
