# Business Protection Guide

## Types of Business Protection

### 1. Keyman Insurance

**Purpose**: Compensate business for financial loss from death/illness of key person

**Key Person**: Someone whose loss would significantly impact business
- Owner/director
- Key sales person
- Technical specialist
- Senior manager with unique knowledge

#### Calculating Keyman Cover

**Method 1: Profit Contribution**
```
Key Person's Contribution to Annual Profit × Recovery Period
```
Example: £100,000 profit contribution × 5 years = £500,000

**Method 2: Cost of Replacement**
```
Recruitment Cost + Training Cost + Lost Productivity Period
```
Example: £50,000 + £30,000 + £150,000 = £230,000

**Method 3: Multiple of Salary**
```
Annual Salary × Multiple (typically 5-10×)
```
Example: £60,000 × 7 = £420,000

**Method 4: Business Valuation Impact**
- What would business sell for with key person?
- What would it sell for without?
- Difference = keyman value

#### Keyman Policy Structure

**Ownership**:
- Company owns policy
- Company pays premiums
- Company is beneficiary

**Tax Treatment**:
- Premiums: Not tax deductible (usually)
- Payout: Tax-free to company
- Corporation Tax: Check current HMRC rules

**Use of Proceeds**:
- Recruit and train replacement
- Cover lost profits during transition
- Pay off debts
- Support business through difficult period

**Types**:
- **Life insurance**: Death of key person
- **Critical illness**: Serious illness of key person
- **Income protection**: Key person unable to work

**Example**:
- Company: IT consultancy, 3 directors
- Key person: Technical director (unique skills)
- Salary: £70,000
- Profit contribution: £150,000/year
- Calculation: £150,000 × 5 years = £750,000
- Policy: £750,000 life + CI
- Owned by company, pays to company on claim

### 2. Shareholder Protection

**Purpose**: Enable surviving shareholders to buy deceased shareholder's shares

**Problem It Solves**:
- Shareholder dies
- Shares pass to estate/family
- Family becomes shareholder (may be unsuitable)
- Surviving shareholders want to buy shares
- But lack capital

**Solution**:
- Life insurance on each shareholder
- Proceeds buy shares from estate
- Funded buyout

#### Structure

**Step 1: Valuation Agreement**
- Agree company valuation method
- Document in shareholders' agreement
- Review annually

**Step 2: Cross-Option Agreement**
- Deceased's estate has option to sell shares
- Surviving shareholders have option to buy shares
- At agreed valuation
- Legally binding

**Step 3: Life Insurance**
- Each shareholder insured
- Sum assured = value of their shareholding
- Surviving shareholders are beneficiaries

**Ownership Models**:

**Model 1: Own Life in Trust**
- Each shareholder owns policy on own life
- Written in trust for other shareholders
- Premiums paid personally

**Model 2: Cross Policies**
- Each shareholder owns policies on other shareholders
- A owns policy on B's life
- B owns policy on A's life

**Example (3 Shareholders)**:
- Company value: £3,000,000
- Shareholder A: 50% (£1,500,000)
- Shareholder B: 30% (£900,000)
- Shareholder C: 20% (£600,000)

**Policies Needed**:
- A's life: £1,500,000 (owned by B and C in proportion)
- B's life: £900,000 (owned by A and C in proportion)
- C's life: £600,000 (owned by A and B in proportion)

**A Dies**:
- B and C claim £1,500,000
- Use proceeds to buy A's shares from estate
- B and C now own 100% of company
- A's family receives cash, not shares

#### Tax Treatment

**Premiums**: Not tax deductible

**Proceeds**: Tax-free to beneficiaries

**Share Purchase**: Capital Gains Tax may apply (but usually covered by reliefs)

**Important**: Seek tax advice for specific circumstances

### 3. Partnership Protection

**Purpose**: Similar to shareholder protection but for partnerships

**Problem**:
- Partner dies
- Deceased's share passes to estate
- Estate becomes partner (unsuitable)
- Surviving partners want to buy out
- But lack capital

**Structure**:
- Similar to shareholder protection
- Cross-option agreement
- Life insurance on each partner
- Proceeds buy partnership share

**Partnership Agreement Requirements**:
- Valuation method
- Buyout procedure
- Cross-option terms
- Restrictive covenants

**Tax Treatment**:
- Similar to shareholder protection
- Premiums not deductible
- Proceeds tax-free
- Purchase may have CGT implications

**Example**:
- Legal partnership, 4 partners
- Firm value: £2,000,000
- Equal 25% shares (£500,000 each)
- Each partner insured for £500,000
- Policies in trust for other partners
- Partner dies: Others claim and buy out deceased's share

### 4. Relevant Life Policy

**Purpose**: Company-paid life insurance for employee/director (tax-efficient)

**How It Works**:
- Company pays premiums
- Cover for employee/director
- Benefit to employee's family (not company)
- Written in trust
- Outside of lifetime allowance

**Tax Benefits**:

**For Company**:
- Premiums allowable expense (corporation tax relief 19-25%)
- No NI on premiums (saving 13.8%)

**For Employee**:
- No P11D benefit in kind (no personal tax)
- No NI charge
- Benefits tax-free to family

**Example**:
- Director, age 40
- Sum assured: £500,000
- Premium: £600/year
- Corporation tax relief (25%): £150
- NI saving (13.8%): £83
- **Net cost to company**: £367/year
- **Benefit to director**: £500k tax-free to family
- **vs Personal Policy**: Director would pay £600 from taxed income (needs to earn £800-£1,000 to net £600)

**Who Benefits Most**:
- Company directors
- Business owners
- High earners (saves more tax)
- Those at/near lifetime allowance

**Limitations**:
- Only for employees (not business owners without employment)
- Cover for individual, not company
- Lost if leave employment (unless transferred)

**vs Group Life Insurance**:
- Relevant Life: Individual policy, portable, no P11D
- Group Life: Scheme for all employees, part of benefits package

---

### 5. Key Person Income Protection

**Purpose**: Replace lost income if key person unable to work

**How It Works**:
- Company owns policy
- Covers key person's inability to work
- Monthly benefit to company
- Company continues to pay key person's salary

**Benefit Calculation**:
- Based on key person's salary or profit contribution
- Typically 50-70% of salary

**Use of Benefit**:
- Pay key person's salary during absence
- Hire temporary replacement
- Cover reduced productivity

**Tax Treatment**:
- Premiums: Corporation tax relief
- Benefits: Taxable as trading receipts

**Example**:
- Sales director, salary £80,000
- Generates £200,000 profit/year
- IP benefit: £4,000/month
- Deferred period: 13 weeks
- Company receives £4,000/month if director unable to work
- Uses to maintain sales function

## Business Protection Needs Analysis

### Step 1: Identify Key People

**Questions**:
- Whose loss would significantly impact profits?
- Who has unique skills or knowledge?
- Who drives revenue?
- Who are key client relationships dependent on?

**Typical Key People**:
- Business owner(s)
- Sales director
- Technical specialist
- Key account managers

### Step 2: Calculate Impact

**For Each Key Person**:
- What profits do they generate?
- How long to replace them?
- What's the cost of replacement?
- What's the impact on business value?

### Step 3: Calculate Share Values

**For Shareholders/Partners**:
- Current business valuation
- Each person's shareholding %
- Value of each shareholding
- Funding required to buy out

**Valuation Methods**:
- **Net Asset Value**: Assets less liabilities
- **Multiple of Earnings**: Profits × industry multiple
- **Discounted Cash Flow**: Future cash flows discounted
- **Professional Valuation**: Accountant or valuer

**Example**:
- Net profits: £300,000
- Industry multiple: 5×
- Valuation: £1,500,000
- 2 equal shareholders: £750,000 each
- Cover needed: £750,000 per shareholder

### Step 4: Review Existing Cover

**Check**:
- Any existing keyman insurance?
- Any shareholder protection in place?
- Group life insurance?
- Personal life insurance (different purpose)

### Step 5: Recommend Solutions

**Typical Package**:
1. Keyman insurance (key individuals)
2. Shareholder/partnership protection (all owners)
3. Relevant life policies (directors)
4. Key person IP (very key individuals)

## Legal Agreements Required

### Shareholders' Agreement

**Must Include**:
- Valuation methodology
- How often to review valuation
- Buyout procedure on death
- Funding mechanism (life insurance)
- Dispute resolution
- Restrictive covenants

**Review**: Annually (at minimum)

### Partnership Agreement

**Must Include**:
- Similar to shareholders' agreement
- Partnership valuation
- Buyout on death/retirement
- Incoming partners provisions

### Cross-Option Agreement

**Grants**:
- Option for deceased's estate to require purchase
- Option for survivors to require sale
- At pre-agreed valuation
- Funded by life insurance

**Legally Binding**: Essential for protection to work

### Shareholder Protection Deed

**Documents**:
- Who is insured
- Sum assured for each
- Who owns policies
- Trust arrangements
- Buyout procedure

**Must Align**: With shareholders' agreement

## Tax Considerations

### Premiums

**Keyman Insurance**:
- Generally not tax deductible
- Exception: If meets HMRC "wholly and exclusively" test
- Rare to get relief

**Shareholder/Partnership Protection**:
- Not tax deductible (capital purpose)
- Personal expense even if company pays

**Relevant Life**:
- Tax deductible (allowable expense)
- Corporation tax relief
- No NI

**Key Person IP**:
- Tax deductible
- Corporation tax relief

### Payouts

**Keyman Insurance**:
- Tax-free to company (usually)
- But check current HMRC rules

**Shareholder/Partnership Protection**:
- Proceeds tax-free to beneficiaries
- Share purchase may have CGT implications
- Business Property Relief may apply (IHT)

**Relevant Life**:
- Payout tax-free to family
- Outside estate (if in trust)

**Key Person IP**:
- Taxable as trading receipts

**Important**: Tax rules change - always seek current tax advice

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Small Ltd Company (2 Directors)

**Scenario**:
- IT Company, 2 directors, equal shareholders
- Company value: £1,000,000
- Each director crucial to business
- Salaries: £60,000 each

**Recommendation**:
1. **Shareholder protection**: £500,000 each (buy shares on death)
2. **Keyman insurance**: £300,000 each (cover profit loss)
3. **Relevant life**: £500,000 each (personal family protection)

**Total per director**: 3 policies
**Cost**: ~£150-200/month total (company pays)

### Example 2: Partnership (4 Partners)

**Scenario**:
- Legal firm, 4 partners
- Firm value: £2,400,000
- Equal 25% shares (£600,000 each)
- Generate fee income together

**Recommendation**:
1. **Partnership protection**: £600,000 each
   - Policies in trust for other partners
   - Cross-option agreement in place
2. **Partnership IP**: Cover firm income if partner absent

**Total**: 4 × £600k policies (£2.4m total cover)

### Example 3: Larger Company (5 Directors, 20 Employees)

**Scenario**:
- Manufacturing, 5 directors (unequal shares)
- 20 employees
- Turnover: £5,000,000, profit: £500,000

**Recommendation**:
1. **Shareholder protection**: Each director's shareholding
   - Director A (40%): £800,000
   - Director B (30%): £600,000
   - Director C-E (10% each): £200,000 each
2. **Keyman**: CEO and Sales Director (£500k each)
3. **Relevant Life**: All 5 directors
4. **Group Life**: 4× salary for all employees (death in service)

## Review and Maintenance

### Annual Review Essential

**Review**:
- Business valuation (has it changed?)
- Shareholdings (any changes?)
- Key people (still key? New key people?)
- Cover amounts (still adequate?)
- Premium affordability

**Triggers for Review**:
- New shareholder joins
- Shareholder leaves
- Business grows significantly
- Business valuation changes materially
- Change in business structure

### Common Mistakes

**1. No Legal Agreements**:
- Insurance without agreement doesn't work
- Must have shareholders' agreement and cross-option

**2. Underinsured**:
- Haven't kept up with business growth
- Cover based on old valuation

**3. No Trust**:
- Shareholder protection not in trust
- Proceeds go to wrong people

**4. Wrong Ownership**:
- Company owns shareholder protection (wrong)
- Should be owned by shareholders in trust

**5. No Review**:
- Set up 10 years ago, never reviewed
- Business completely different now

## Best Practice

**Essential Steps**:
1. Conduct thorough business protection needs analysis
2. Obtain professional business valuation
3. Draft shareholders'/partnership agreement (solicitor)
4. Set up cross-option agreement
5. Arrange appropriate life insurance
6. Write policies in trust (shareholder protection)
7. Review annually
8. Update when business changes
9. Seek tax advice (rules change)
10. Document everything clearly
