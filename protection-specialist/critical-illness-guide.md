# Critical Illness Guide

## What is Critical Illness Cover?

**Purpose**: Pays a tax-free lump sum if diagnosed with a specified serious illness

**Not**: Health insurance (which pays treatment costs)
**Is**: Financial protection against loss of income and increased costs

## Core Conditions (Big Three)

All providers cover these:

### 1. Cancer

**Standard Definition** (most providers):
- Malignant tumor
- Characterized by uncontrolled growth and spread
- Requiring chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or surgery

**Exclusions** (varies by provider):
- Skin cancers (except malignant melanoma)
- Prostate cancer (T1 N0 M0 or below)
- Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (early stage)
- Carcinoma in situ (pre-cancerous)

**Provider Variations**:
- **Better**: Pays for all cancers (including in situ)
- **Standard**: Excludes less serious cancers
- **Budget**: More exclusions

### 2. Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction)

**Standard Definition**:
- Death of heart muscle
- Due to inadequate blood supply
- New ECG changes AND
- Rise in cardiac enzymes

**Provider Variations**:
- Some require all diagnostic criteria
- Others more lenient (2 out of 3)
- Severity thresholds differ

**What's NOT Covered**:
- Angina (without heart attack)
- Raised troponin without other evidence
- Minor myocardial damage

### 3. Stroke

**Standard Definition**:
- Cerebrovascular accident/incident
- Resulting in permanent neurological deficit
- Lasting >24 hours
- Confirmed by neurologist

**Severity Requirements**:
- Must result in permanent symptoms
- Transient ischemic attack (TIA) excluded
- Mini-stroke excluded unless permanent damage

**Provider Variations**:
- **Strict**: Permanent and significant disability
- **Moderate**: Permanent symptoms (any level)
- **Better**: Includes some TIAs if treatment needed

## Additional Conditions

Providers typically cover 30-60+ conditions:

### Common Additional Conditions

**Cardiovascular**:
- Coronary artery bypass surgery
- Heart valve replacement
- Aortic surgery
- Pulmonary artery surgery

**Neurological**:
- Multiple sclerosis
- Motor neurone disease
- Parkinson's disease
- Alzheimer's disease
- Benign brain tumor

**Organ Failure**:
- Kidney failure (permanent dialysis)
- Liver failure
- Respiratory failure
- Heart failure

**Other Serious Conditions**:
- Loss of limbs
- Loss of sight
- Loss of hearing
- Paralysis
- Coma
- Major organ transplant
- Third-degree burns
- Total permanent disability

### Children's Conditions

Many policies include cover for dependent children:

**Typical Children's Cover**:
- Cancer
- Bacterial meningitis
- Muscular dystrophy
- Cerebral palsy
- Cystic fibrosis
- Deaf, blind, or loss of limbs
- Type 1 diabetes (severe)

**Benefit**: Usually £10,000-£25,000 (included free or small additional premium)

## Policy Structures

### 1. Standalone Critical Illness

**Features**:
- CI cover only
- No life insurance element
- Pays on diagnosis
- Policy continues if claimed (rare)

**Use Cases**:
- Already have life insurance
- CI-specific need
- Separate trust arrangements

**Rare**: Most CI sold with life insurance

### 2. Life Insurance + Critical Illness (Accelerated)

**Features**:
- Combined policy
- Pays on first event (death OR CI)
- Policy ends after claim
- Most common structure

**Example**:
- £200,000 cover
- Diagnosed with cancer: Pays £200,000, policy ends
- No further life cover after CI claim

**Pros**:
- Cheaper than separate policies
- One policy to manage
- Comprehensive protection

**Cons**:
- Lose life cover if CI claimed
- Might need additional life cover

### 3. Life Insurance + Critical Illness (Additional)

**Features**:
- CI benefit on top of life insurance
- CI claim doesn't end life cover
- Pays once on CI, separately on death
- More expensive

**Example**:
- Life cover: £200,000
- CI cover: £200,000 (additional)
- Claimed CI: Pays £200,000, life cover continues
- Later death: Pays £200,000 again

**Use Cases**:
- High earners
- Comprehensive protection needed
- Can afford higher premiums

### 4. Critical Illness with Buy-Back

**Features**:
- Accelerated benefit
- Option to reinstate life cover after CI claim
- Pay additional premium to buy back
- Guaranteed (no underwriting)

**How It Works**:
1. Diagnosed with CI: Claim £200k
2. Policy ends
3. Option to reinstate £200k life cover
4. Pay higher premium (based on new health status)
5. Guaranteed acceptance

**Pros**:
- Protects against both events
- No underwriting for buy-back
- Flexibility

**Cons**:
- Buy-back premium can be high
- Additional cost may be unaffordable when ill

## Definition Quality

**Critical Factor**: Provider definitions vary significantly

### What to Check

**1. Severity Requirements**
- Some require "permanent total disability"
- Others pay on diagnosis with "permanent symptoms"
- Lower severity = better for client

**2. Diagnostic Criteria**
- How many tests required?
- Who must diagnose (GP vs specialist)?
- How long must symptoms last?

**3. Exclusions**
- What's specifically excluded?
- Early-stage cancers covered?
- Pre-existing conditions?

### Defaqto Ratings

**5 Star**: Most comprehensive
- Broadest definitions
- Lowest severity thresholds
- Fewest exclusions
- Additional conditions

**3-4 Star**: Standard market
- Industry-standard definitions
- Typical exclusions
- Core conditions well covered

**1-2 Star**: Budget
- Stricter definitions
- More exclusions
- Higher severity thresholds

## Partial and Additional Payment Benefits

### Partial Payment Conditions

Some providers pay percentage for less serious conditions:

**Examples**:
- Early-stage cancer: 25% of sum assured
- Angioplasty: 25%
- Less serious heart attack: 50%

**Benefit**:
- Provides support for less serious but still significant illness
- Policy continues for full claim later

### Additional Payment Benefits

**Hospital Cash Benefit**:
- £50-£100 per night in hospital
- Maximum 30-60 nights
- No cost to policy

**Rehabilitation Benefit**:
- Up to £25,000 for rehabilitation
- After valid CI claim
- Physio, counseling, adaptations

**Second Opinion Service**:
- Access to medical specialists
- For diagnosis confirmation
- Included free

**Premium Back on Death**:
- If no CI claim and die
- Return of premiums paid
- Significantly increases premium (not recommended)

## Sum Assured Considerations

**Typical Amounts**:
- Match mortgage: £100k-£300k
- Income replacement: 3-5× salary
- Comprehensive: Mortgage + income + adaptations

**Use Cases for CI Payout**:
- Clear mortgage (reduce stress)
- Adapt home (wheelchair access, stairlift)
- Reduce working hours
- Private medical care
- Cover partner reducing hours to care
- Clear debts
- Financial cushion during treatment

**Too Little**:
- Won't clear mortgage or provide meaningful support
- Minimum recommended: £50k

**Too Much**:
- Unaffordable premiums
- Over-insurance

## Cost Factors

**Age**: Biggest factor
- Age 30: £10-15/month per £100k
- Age 50: £50-70/month per £100k

**Gender**: Women more expensive
- Women claim more frequently
- Especially younger ages

**Smoker**: +50-100% premium
- Defined as any tobacco in last 12 months
- E-cigarettes count as smoker for most

**Health**: Loadings or exclusions
- Family history (cancer, heart disease)
- BMI outside normal range
- Medical conditions

**Occupation**: Minimal impact
- Unlike life insurance
- Manual work slight increase

**Sum Assured**: Linear
- £100k = £20/month
- £200k = £40/month

**Term**: Longer = more expensive
- Age 65 much more than age 60
- Most claims age 50-60

## Claims Statistics

**Average Claims Paid**:
- Cancer: 60-70% of all CI claims
- Heart attack: 10-15%
- Stroke: 5-10%
- Other: 10-25%

**Claims Ratios** (by provider):
- Best providers: 90-95% paid
- Average: 85-90% paid
- Poor: <80% paid

**Decline Reasons**:
- Doesn't meet severity threshold
- Non-disclosure of pre-existing condition
- Excluded condition
- Claim outside definition

**Average Time to Claim**: 7-10 years into policy

## Critical Illness vs Income Protection

| Factor | Critical Illness | Income Protection |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Trigger** | Diagnosis of specific illness | Unable to work (any illness/injury) |
| **Payment** | Lump sum | Monthly income |
| **Coverage** | 30-60 specific conditions | Any illness/injury preventing work |
| **Claim Probability** | Lower (~15-20% lifetime) | Higher (~30-40% lifetime) |
| **Cost** | More expensive | Less expensive (term for term) |
| **Flexibility** | Lump sum can be used for anything | Income replacement only |

**When CI Better**:
- Lump sum need (mortgage, debts)
- Want payment certainty
- Limited budget (take both if possible)

**When IP Better**:
- Income replacement priority
- Ongoing expenses concern
- Broader protection needed

**Ideal**: Both CI and IP (complementary)

## Common Exclusions and Limitations

**Pre-Existing Conditions**:
- Must disclose all medical history
- Pre-existing conditions usually excluded
- Or policy voided if non-disclosure discovered

**Waiting Period**:
- Usually none for CI
- But may be 30-90 days for some conditions

**Age Limits**:
- Entry age: Up to 60-65
- Expiry age: Usually 65-70

**Survival Period**:
- Some require survival 14-30 days after diagnosis
- To prevent death benefit overlap
- Check policy terms

**Geographic Limits**:
- Usually worldwide cover
- Treatment must be by recognized medical professional

## Underwriting Process

**Application Questions**:
- Family history (parents, siblings)
- Personal medical history
- Lifestyle (smoking, alcohol, drugs)
- Height and weight (BMI)
- Occupation and hobbies

**Underwriting Outcomes**:

**Standard Terms**:
- Accepted at standard rates
- No exclusions

**Exclusion**:
- Specific condition excluded
- e.g., "All claims relating to your back condition are excluded"
- Premium reduced

**Loading**:
- Increased premium (+25%, +50%, +100%, etc.)
- All conditions covered
- Permanent loading

**Postponement**:
- Recent diagnosis or awaiting results
- Reapply in 6-12 months
- Treatment completed and stable

**Decline**:
- Uninsurable for CI
- Consider Income Protection instead
- Guaranteed acceptance life insurance (for death cover)

## Suitability Considerations

**CI is Suitable When**:
- Client has dependents or mortgage
- Concerned about impact of serious illness
- Can afford premiums
- Understands limitations and exclusions
- Needs complement life insurance

**CI May NOT Be Suitable When**:
- Very limited budget (prioritize life insurance or IP)
- Already significant savings
- No mortgage or dependents
- Health conditions make it unaffordable
- Don't understand product

**Better Alternatives (if unsuitable)**:
- Income Protection (ongoing income vs lump sum)
- Increased life insurance
- Savings plan
- Employer sick pay and death benefits

## Review and Replacement

**When Reviewing Existing CI**:
- Definitions improved over time
- Claims statistics vary by provider
- Premiums may be better for new applicants
- But: Now older, health may have changed

**Replacement Considerations**:
- New underwriting required
- Current health status
- Age increase = higher premium
- Loss of policy history
- Any loadings or exclusions

**When to Replace**:
- Significantly better definitions
- Much lower premium for same cover
- Current policy poor provider
- Additional benefits valuable

**When NOT to Replace**:
- Health has deteriorated (likely decline/loading)
- Minimal benefit to changing
- Close to claim age (50+)
- Existing policy has loadings (new one likely worse)

## Tax Treatment

**Premiums**: Not tax deductible

**Payouts**: Tax-free lump sum
- No income tax
- No capital gains tax
- No IHT (if in trust or paid to individual)

**Business Use**: Corporation tax relief if business pays

## Common Client Questions

**"Does it cover COVID-19?"**
- If results in permanent organ damage (e.g., respiratory failure, heart damage)
- Not for mild/moderate cases that recover
- Check provider specific stance

**"What if I have two conditions?"**
- Policy pays once only (accelerated benefit)
- Unless "additional benefit" structure
- First diagnosis triggers claim

**"Can I claim for mental health?"**
- Usually not
- Possible for severe organic brain damage
- Income Protection better for mental health

**"Do I need CI if I have income protection?"**
- Complementary products
- CI: Lump sum for serious conditions
- IP: Income for any inability to work
- Both together = comprehensive protection

## Red Flags (Poor Advice)

- CI recommended without explaining IP alternative
- Sum assured not justified by needs analysis
- No comparison of provider definitions
- Accelerated benefit without considering impact of losing life cover
- Premium back options (expensive, poor value)
- Recommending CI when IP would suit better

## Best Practice

**Good CI advice includes**:
- Clear needs analysis (why lump sum needed)
- Comparison of definitions (Defaqto ratings)
- Claims statistics considered
- Alternative products considered (IP)
- Sum assured justified
- Budget affordability checked
- Limitations and exclusions explained
- Trust recommendation
- Regular review arranged
