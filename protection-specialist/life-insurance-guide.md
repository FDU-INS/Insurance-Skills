# Life Insurance Guide

## Types of Life Insurance

### 1. Term Assurance

**Overview**: Cover for a set period (term). Pays out only if death occurs during term.

#### Level Term Assurance

**Features**:
- Fixed sum assured throughout term
- Premium usually guaranteed
- No cash value
- Cheapest form of life insurance

**Use Cases**:
- Income replacement
- Debt coverage (not decreasing)
- Fixed financial need
- Business protection

**Example**:
- Sum assured: £300,000
- Term: 25 years
- Premium: £15/month
- Pays £300,000 if death occurs within 25 years

#### Decreasing Term Assurance

**Features**:
- Sum assured reduces over time
- Usually reduces in line with repayment mortgage
- Lower premiums than level term
- No cash value

**Use Cases**:
- Repayment mortgage protection
- Loan repayment
- Reducing liability

**Example**:
- Initial sum assured: £200,000
- Reduces to £0 over 25 years
- Premium: £8/month
- Matches mortgage reducing balance

#### Increasing Term Assurance

**Features**:
- Sum assured increases annually (e.g., 3% or RPI)
- Protects against inflation
- Higher initial premium
- Premium usually increases with cover

**Use Cases**:
- Long-term income replacement
- Inflation protection
- Future financial needs

**Example**:
- Initial sum assured: £250,000
- Increases: 3% per year
- After 10 years: £336,000
- After 20 years: £451,000

#### Renewable Term

**Features**:
- Option to renew at end of term
- No medical evidence required
- Premium increases at renewal (based on age)
- Guarantees future insurability

**Use Cases**:
- Uncertain future needs
- Short-term with possible extension
- Health concerns (lock in insurability)

#### Convertible Term

**Features**:
- Option to convert to whole of life
- No medical evidence required
- Usually within first 10 years
- Guarantees future cover

**Use Cases**:
- Young clients expecting future needs
- Temporary budget constraints
- Future IHT planning potential

### 2. Whole of Life Assurance

**Overview**: Cover for entire life. Pays out on death whenever it occurs.

#### Investment-Based (Unit-Linked)

**Features**:
- Premium invested in funds
- Sum assured linked to fund value
- Reviewable premiums
- Can build cash value
- More expensive than term

**How It Works**:
- Part of premium pays for life cover
- Remainder invested in funds
- Fund growth intended to keep premiums level
- If fund underperforms, premiums increase

**Use Cases**:
- Inheritance tax planning
- Lifetime protection need
- Estate planning
- Funeral costs

**Risks**:
- Premium reviews (can increase significantly)
- Fund underperformance
- Complexity

#### Guaranteed Whole of Life

**Features**:
- Fixed premiums for life
- Guaranteed sum assured
- No investment element
- More expensive but certain

**Use Cases**:
- IHT planning (known liability)
- Certainty required
- No risk tolerance

**Example**:
- Sum assured: £100,000
- Premium: £85/month (age 50)
- Guaranteed payable on death
- No reviews, no increases

#### Maximum Cover (Guaranteed Acceptance)

**Features**:
- No medical underwriting
- Usually age 50-80
- Lower sum assured (£10,000-£25,000)
- Graded benefits (first 1-2 years)
- Higher premiums

**Use Cases**:
- Funeral cost planning
- Uninsurable clients
- Simple needs, older clients

**Graded Benefits**:
- Year 1: Return of premiums only (if non-accidental death)
- Year 2: 50% of sum assured
- Year 3+: Full sum assured
- Accidental death: Full benefit from day 1

### 3. Family Income Benefit (FIB)

**Overview**: Pays regular income instead of lump sum if death occurs.

**Features**:
- Monthly or annual income
- Paid from death until end of term
- Usually cheaper than lump sum equivalent
- Reduces over time (less years to pay)

**Use Cases**:
- Income replacement focus
- Budgeting for dependents
- Prevents lump sum mismanagement
- Regular expenses coverage

**Example**:
- Benefit: £2,000/month
- Term: 20 years
- Death in year 5: Pays £2,000/month for 15 years (£360,000 total)
- Death in year 18: Pays £2,000/month for 2 years (£48,000 total)

**FIB vs Level Term Comparison**:
| Factor | FIB | Level Term |
|--------|-----|------------|
| Payment | Regular income | Lump sum |
| Cost | Usually cheaper | More expensive |
| Flexibility | Less (fixed income) | More (can invest as needed) |
| Mismanagement Risk | Lower | Higher |
| Investment Opportunity | None | Lump sum can be invested |

### 4. Relevant Life Policies

**Overview**: Company-paid life insurance for employees/directors.

**Features**:
- Company pays premiums (allowable expense)
- No P11D benefit in kind
- Pays to dependents (not company)
- Written in trust
- Not subject to lifetime allowance

**Tax Benefits**:
- Corporation tax relief on premiums (19-25%)
- No personal tax charge for employee
- No NI on premiums (13.8% employer saving)
- IHT-efficient (outside estate)

**Use Cases**:
- Company directors
- Key employees
- Business owners
- Tax-efficient remuneration

**Example**:
- Sum assured: £500,000
- Annual premium: £600
- Corporation tax relief: £150 (25%)
- NI saving: £83
- Net cost to company: £367
- Employee receives: £500k tax-free to family

**vs Group Life Insurance**:
- Relevant Life: Individual policy, portable
- Group Life: Employer scheme, lost if leave

## Policy Features and Options

### Indexation (Premium Increases)

**What It Is**: Annual increase in cover (and premium)

**Options**:
- **RPI**: Retail Price Index
- **CPI**: Consumer Price Index
- **Fixed %**: e.g., 3% or 5% per year

**Pros**:
- Maintains real value of cover
- Protects against inflation
- Automatic (no underwriting)

**Cons**:
- Premiums increase annually
- Can become unaffordable
- May increase beyond need

**When to Use**:
- Long terms (15+ years)
- Income replacement focus
- Inflation concern

### Waiver of Premium

**What It Is**: Premiums paid if you're unable to work

**How It Works**:
- If off work >6 months (typically)
- Insurer pays your premiums
- Cover continues
- Until return to work or age 60-65

**Cost**: Usually 10-15% of base premium

**When to Use**:
- If no income protection
- Concerned about affording premiums if ill
- Long-term policies

**vs Income Protection**:
- IP: Pays income benefit
- Waiver: Just pays your life insurance premium
- Waiver much cheaper but limited benefit

### Terminal Illness Benefit

**What It Is**: Early payment if diagnosed with <12 months to live

**Features**:
- Usually included free
- Medical evidence required
- Pays sum assured early
- Policy then ends

**Use Cases**:
- Debt repayment before death
- Quality of life improvements
- Final expenses
- Family support

**Criteria** (varies by provider):
- Life expectancy <12 months
- Two doctors confirm
- Claim before age 75 (usually)

### Total Permanent Disability (TPD)

**What It Is**: Pays if permanently unable to work

**Definitions**:
- **Own occupation**: Can't do your job
- **Any occupation**: Can't do any job
- **Activities of Daily Living**: Can't perform basic tasks

**Less Common**: More common in critical illness policies

**Cost**: Additional premium (significant)

## Sum Assured Calculation

See needs-analysis-framework.md for detailed methods.

**Quick Reference**:
- **Debt coverage**: Outstanding debts + funeral (£5k)
- **Income replacement**: Annual income × years × 60-70%
- **Family protection**: £250k-£500k typical
- **Mortgage only**: Outstanding balance
- **Funeral costs only**: £10k-£15k

## Term Length Selection

**How to Choose Term**:

**Mortgage Protection**:
- Match mortgage term
- Repayment: Decreasing term
- Interest-only: Level term

**Income Replacement**:
- Until youngest child independent (age 18-21)
- Until retirement age
- Fixed period (10, 15, 20, 25 years)

**Business Protection**:
- Until retirement
- Until partnership dissolves
- Until business sold

**General Guidance**:
- Longer term = higher premium
- But per-year cost lower
- Review at major life events

## Single vs Joint Life Policies

### Single Life

**Features**:
- Covers one person
- Pays on that person's death
- Premium based on one life

**Use Cases**:
- Each partner needs different cover
- Different terms needed
- Different trust arrangements
- Divorce/separation possible

**Example**:
- Policy 1: Husband, £300k, 25 years
- Policy 2: Wife, £200k, 20 years

### Joint Life First Death

**Features**:
- Covers two people
- Pays on first death
- Policy then ends
- Usually cheaper than two single policies

**Use Cases**:
- Mortgage protection (one death clears)
- Equal needs
- Married couples
- Cost saving

**Example**:
- Joint life £250k, 25 years
- Premium: £20/month
- vs Two singles: £15 + £12 = £27/month
- Saving: £7/month

**Drawback**:
- Survivor left uninsured after claim
- Can't separate if divorce
- Less flexible

### Joint Life Second Death

**Features**:
- Pays on second death
- Both must die for claim
- Usually for IHT planning

**Use Cases**:
- Inheritance tax (liability on second death)
- Estate planning
- Older couples

**Less Common**: Niche product

## Underwriting Classes

**Standard Rates**:
- Good health
- Non-smoker
- No hazardous occupation/hobbies
- BMI normal range

**Loadings** (increased premiums):
- Smoker: +50-100%
- Medical history: +25-200%
- Occupation: +25-100%
- Hobbies: +50-200%

**Exclusions**:
- Specific condition excluded
- e.g., heart condition excluded from CI
- Policy cheaper but limited

**Postponement**:
- Recent diagnosis
- Await test results
- Review in 6-12 months

**Decline**:
- Uninsurable
- Seek specialist provider
- Consider guaranteed acceptance products

## Provider Comparison Factors

**Premium**:
- Most obvious factor
- But not only factor
- Compare like-for-like

**Financial Strength**:
- Will they pay in 25 years?
- Check ratings (S&P, Fitch, Moody's)
- Long-established providers

**Claims Philosophy**:
- % of claims paid
- Ease of claims process
- Reputation

**Policy Features**:
- Terminal illness included?
- Conversion options?
- Guaranteed premiums?

**Service Quality**:
- Application process speed
- Online management
- Customer service

## Common Combinations

### Young Family Package

**Scenario**: Married, 2 young children, mortgage

**Recommendation**:
1. Joint life first death: £250k (mortgage)
2. Single life (main earner): £250k (income replacement)
3. Single life (second earner): £150k (childcare/income)
4. Optional: Critical illness on one or both

### Single Person, Mortgage

**Scenario**: Single, mortgage, no dependents

**Recommendation**:
1. Decreasing term: Mortgage balance
2. Optional: Small level term for funeral/debts (£25k)

### Business Owner

**Scenario**: Company director, family, shareholder

**Recommendation**:
1. Relevant life: £500k (family protection, tax-efficient)
2. Shareholder protection: Business valuation × shareholding
3. Personal life insurance: Additional if needed

### Pre-Retirement

**Scenario**: Age 55, children independent, small mortgage

**Recommendation**:
1. Short-term term: 5-10 years (mortgage/income)
2. Whole of life: £100k (funeral, IHT planning)
3. Trust arrangement for IHT efficiency

## Tax Treatment

**Premiums**:
- Not tax deductible (personal)
- Corporation tax relief (relevant life)

**Payouts**:
- Tax-free to beneficiaries
- But could be subject to IHT if not in trust
- No income tax or CGT

**Inheritance Tax**:
- If not in trust: Forms part of estate
- If in trust: Outside estate (no IHT)
- **Always recommend trust**

## Review Triggers

**Review life insurance when**:
- Marriage/partnership
- Divorce/separation
- Birth of child
- House purchase/remortgage
- Salary increase/decrease
- New debts
- Existing policy ending soon
- Every 3-5 years minimum
- Retirement approaching

## Common Client Questions

**"Can I reduce my cover?"**
- Usually yes (but check policy)
- Premium reduces proportionately
- No underwriting needed

**"Can I increase my cover?"**
- New medical evidence required
- Easier to take new policy
- Conversion options if available

**"What if I stop paying?"**
- Cover ceases
- No refund (term insurance)
- May have 30-day grace period

**"Can I cancel?"**
- Yes, anytime
- 30-day cooling off (full refund)
- No surrender value (term)

**"What if I go abroad?"**
- Usually covered worldwide
- Check policy wording
- War/terrorism may be excluded

## Red Flags (Poor Advice)

- Whole of life when term suits
- Over-insurance (unaffordable)
- Single life when joint cheaper
- Not considering trusts
- Ignoring existing cover
- No needs analysis documented

## Remember

**Good life insurance advice**:
- Based on clear needs analysis
- Suitable type and term
- Affordable and sustainable
- Written in trust (if appropriate)
- Regularly reviewed
- Client understands fully
