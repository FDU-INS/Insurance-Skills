# Life Insurance Planning Guide

Comprehensive guide to life insurance needs analysis, product selection, and underwriting for insurance specialists.

## Life Insurance Needs Analysis

### Human Life Value Method

**Concept:** Present value of individual's future earnings

**Formula:**
```
Human Life Value = Annual Income × Working Years Remaining × Present Value Factor
```

**Example:**
```
Age: 35
Annual Income: $100,000
Years to Retirement: 30
Discount Rate: 4%

Present Value Factor (30 years, 4%): 17.292

Human Life Value = $100,000 × 17.292 = $1,729,200
```

**Advantages:**
- Simple calculation
- Objective (based on income)

**Disadvantages:**
- Conservative (doesn't account for income growth)
- Doesn't consider specific needs
- May understate need

### Needs-Based Analysis

**More Comprehensive Approach - Calculate Specific Needs:**

**1. Income Replacement**
- Years of support needed
- Annual income to replace (typically 60-80% of gross)
- Until children independent or spouse retirement age

**2. Debt Payoff**
- Mortgage balance
- Car loans
- Credit cards
- Student loans
- Business debts

**3. Final Expenses**
- Funeral costs: $7,000-$12,000
- Burial/cremation: $3,000-$8,000
- Estate settlement: $5,000-$10,000
- Total: $15,000-$30,000

**4. Education Funding**
- College costs per child
- Public vs private
- 4 years per child
- Current cost: $100,000-$300,000+ per child

**5. Emergency Fund**
- 6-12 months of expenses
- Transition period for surviving spouse

**6. Special Needs**
- Care for disabled child
- Elderly parent support
- Other obligations

**Less: Existing Resources**
- Existing life insurance (group and individual)
- Savings and investments
- Social Security survivor benefits
- Spouse's income (if applicable)

### Detailed Needs Analysis Example

**Client Profile:**
- Age 40, married, two children (ages 8 and 10)
- Annual income: $150,000
- Spouse income: $50,000
- Mortgage balance: $400,000
- Car loans: $30,000
- Savings: $50,000
- Existing group life insurance: $300,000
- 401(k): $250,000

**Needs Calculation:**

```
Income Replacement:
- Replace $150,000 income for 20 years (until children independent and spouse retires)
- 70% replacement ratio = $105,000/year
- $105,000 × 20 years = $2,100,000

Debt Payoff:
- Mortgage: $400,000
- Car loans: $30,000
- Total: $430,000

Final Expenses: $20,000

Education Funding:
- Child 1 (in 8 years): $200,000
- Child 2 (in 10 years): $200,000
- Total: $400,000

Emergency Fund:
- 12 months expenses = $90,000

TOTAL NEED: $3,040,000

Less Existing Resources:
- Group life insurance: $300,000
- Savings: $50,000
- 401(k): $250,000
- Spouse income (assume covers own living expenses): $0
- Social Security survivors (approx $3,000/month x 20 years): $720,000
Total Resources: $1,320,000

NET INSURANCE NEED: $3,040,000 - $1,320,000 = $1,720,000

RECOMMENDATION: $1,750,000 life insurance policy
```

### Capital Needs Analysis (Income Approach)

**Calculate lump sum needed to generate required income**

**Formula:**
```
Capital Needed = Annual Income Need ÷ Safe Withdrawal Rate
```

**Example:**
```
Annual income need after death: $100,000
Safe withdrawal rate: 4%

Capital Needed = $100,000 ÷ 0.04 = $2,500,000
```

**Considerations:**
- Social Security survivor benefits (reduce need)
- Spouse's income (reduce need)
- Existing assets (reduce need)
- Inflation (increase need over time)

## Life Insurance Products

### Term Life Insurance

**Concept:** Pure death benefit protection for specified term

**Types:**

**Level Term:**
- Death benefit remains constant
- Premium remains constant for term
- Most common: 10, 15, 20, 30 years
- Premium increases significantly at renewal

**Decreasing Term:**
- Death benefit decreases over time
- Premium typically level
- Often matches mortgage amortization
- Less common than level term

**Annual Renewable Term (ART):**
- 1-year term, automatically renews
- Premium increases each year
- Expensive in later years

**Features:**

**Convertibility:**
- Convert to permanent policy without medical exam
- Usually within first 10-20 years
- Important if health declines

**Renewability:**
- Renew at end of term without medical exam
- Premium based on attained age (much higher)

**Return of Premium (ROP) Rider:**
- Returns all premiums if outlive term
- Expensive (50-100%+ higher premium)
- May provide lower return than investing difference

**Advantages:**
- Lowest cost for pure death benefit
- Simple to understand
- Flexible term lengths

**Disadvantages:**
- No cash value
- Coverage ends at term expiration
- Renewal expensive

**Best For:**
- Temporary needs (mortgage, children's dependency period)
- Budget-conscious clients
- Term certain obligations

**Cost Example (Age 40, $1M death benefit, 20-year term):**
```
Healthy non-smoker male: $50-$70/month
Healthy non-smoker female: $40-$55/month

Total cost over 20 years: $12,000-$16,800
```

### Whole Life Insurance

**Concept:** Permanent coverage with guaranteed cash value buildup

**Features:**

**Guaranteed Death Benefit:**
- Coverage to age 100-121
- Death benefit never decreases (unless withdraw cash value)

**Cash Value Accumulation:**
- Guaranteed growth at stated rate (typically 2-4%)
- Plus potential dividends (non-guaranteed)
- Builds slowly in early years, accelerates later

**Level Premiums:**
- Fixed premium for life
- Higher than term but never increases

**Dividends (Participating Policies):**
- Mutual insurance companies
- Share of company profits
- Not guaranteed but historically paid
- Options:
  - Cash
  - Reduce premiums
  - Buy paid-up additional insurance
  - Accumulate at interest
  - Term insurance

**Loans:**
- Borrow against cash value
- No credit check
- Interest charged (typically 5-8%)
- Reduces death benefit if not repaid

**Guaranteed vs Non-Guaranteed:**
- Guaranteed elements: Death benefit, minimum cash value, maximum premium
- Non-guaranteed: Dividends, enhanced cash value

**Advantages:**
- Lifetime coverage
- Forced savings
- Cash value guaranteed to grow
- Predictable premiums
- Dividends (if participating)

**Disadvantages:**
- Expensive (10-15x cost of term)
- Cash value builds slowly
- Limited flexibility

**Best For:**
- Lifetime protection needs (estate planning)
- Forced savings/wealth accumulation
- Clients who want guarantees
- Business succession planning

**Cost Example (Age 40, $1M death benefit):**
```
Annual Premium: $10,000-$15,000/year
Total premiums by age 65 (25 years): $250,000-$375,000
Cash value at age 65: Approximately $400,000-$500,000
Death benefit: $1,000,000
```

### Universal Life (UL)

**Concept:** Permanent coverage with flexible premiums and transparent charges

**Structure:**
```
Premium → Minus: Cost of Insurance + Admin Fees → Remainder: Cash Value Account
Cash Value earns interest (declared rate)
```

**Flexibility:**
- **Premiums:** Pay more or less (subject to minimums and maximums)
- **Death Benefit:** Increase or decrease (with underwriting for increases)
- **Withdrawals:** Access cash value

**Interest Crediting:**
- Declared rate set by insurer
- Typically 3-5% current (minimum guaranteed 2-3%)
- Resets periodically

**Cost of Insurance (COI):**
- Charges for death benefit protection
- Increases with age
- Deducted monthly from cash value

**Two Death Benefit Options:**

**Option A (Level Death Benefit):**
- Fixed death benefit
- Cash value reduces net amount at risk
- Lower COI charges

**Option B (Increasing Death Benefit):**
- Death benefit = Face amount + Cash value
- Higher COI charges
- Better for estate planning

**Advantages:**
- Flexibility in premiums
- Transparent charges
- Access to cash value
- Can reduce costs when older

**Disadvantages:**
- Not guaranteed (can lapse if underfunded)
- Requires active management
- Interest rates variable

**Best For:**
- Clients who want flexibility
- Business owners (adjustable premiums)
- Supplemental retirement income

### Indexed Universal Life (IUL)

**Concept:** Universal life with cash value linked to equity index

**Interest Crediting:**
- Based on performance of index (typically S&P 500)
- **Floor:** Minimum guaranteed (typically 0-2%)
- **Cap:** Maximum credited (typically 8-12%)
- **Participation Rate:** Percentage of index return credited (typically 100%)

**Example:**
```
S&P 500 Return: +15%
Cap: 10%
Floor: 0%
Participation: 100%

Credited Interest: 10% (capped at 10%, even though S&P was 15%)

---

S&P 500 Return: -10%
Floor: 0%

Credited Interest: 0% (protected by floor, no loss)
```

**Advantages:**
- Upside potential (vs fixed UL)
- Downside protection (0% floor)
- No direct market exposure

**Disadvantages:**
- Capped upside
- Complexity
- Caps and participation rates can change
- Not guaranteed to stay in force

**Best For:**
- Clients wanting growth potential with protection
- Alternative to whole life for cash accumulation
- Younger clients (long time horizon)

### Variable Universal Life (VUL)

**Concept:** Universal life with cash value invested in sub-accounts (like mutual funds)

**Investment Options:**
- Stock funds
- Bond funds
- Money market
- Balanced funds
- Dozens of choices typically available

**Risk:**
- Cash value fluctuates with investment performance
- Can lose money
- Policyholder bears investment risk

**Securities Product:**
- Requires securities license (Series 6 or 7) to sell
- Prospectus required
- Regulated by SEC and state insurance department

**Advantages:**
- Investment control
- Highest growth potential
- Tax-deferred investment growth

**Disadvantages:**
- Investment risk (can lose cash value)
- Can lapse if poorly performing
- Complex
- Higher fees

**Best For:**
- Sophisticated investors
- Willing to accept risk for growth potential
- Long time horizon

### Variable Life (VL)

**Similar to VUL but with fixed premiums (like whole life)**

**Features:**
- Fixed premiums
- Cash value in sub-accounts
- Minimum guaranteed death benefit
- Securities product

**Advantages:**
- Fixed premiums
- Investment control
- Guaranteed minimum death benefit

**Disadvantages:**
- Investment risk
- Less flexibility than VUL
- Expensive

**Less Common:** Largely replaced by VUL

## Underwriting

### Underwriting Classes

**Preferred Plus / Elite / Super Preferred:**
- Excellent health
- No family history of heart disease, cancer, diabetes
- Ideal height/weight
- Non-smoker
- No risky hobbies
- Lowest premium

**Preferred:**
- Very good health
- Minor family history acceptable
- Good height/weight
- Non-smoker
- Low premium

**Standard Plus:**
- Good health
- Average family history
- Slightly overweight acceptable
- Non-smoker
- Average premium

**Standard:**
- Average health
- May have controlled conditions (high blood pressure, high cholesterol)
- Non-smoker or smoker
- Standard premium

**Table Rated / Substandard:**
- Health impairments
- Tables 1-16 (or A-P)
- Each table adds 25% to premium
- Examples: Diabetes, prior heart attack, cancer survivor

**Declined:**
- Uninsurable
- Very high risk (terminal illness, severe uncontrolled conditions)

**Smoker vs Non-Smoker:**
- Smoker: Any tobacco use in past 12 months
- Non-smoker: No tobacco 12+ months
- Premium difference: 50-150% higher for smokers

### Underwriting Process

**Application:**
- Part 1: General information (name, address, coverage amount, beneficiary)
- Part 2: Medical history (conditions, medications, surgeries, family history)
- Part 3: Financial information (income, net worth, existing coverage)
- Part 4: Lifestyle (hobbies, travel, occupation)

**Medical Exam (if required):**
- Typically required for $250,000+ coverage
- Blood test
- Urinalysis
- Height, weight, blood pressure
- EKG (if age 45+50+ or large policy)
- Conducted by paramedical examiner
- Paid by insurance company

**Medical Records:**
- Attending Physician Statement (APS)
- Insurer requests from doctors
- Reviews medical history

**Medical Information Bureau (MIB):**
- Database of underwriting information
- Insurers report and query
- Helps detect fraud and misrepresentation

**Underwriting Timeline:**
```
Application submitted: Day 0
Medical exam scheduled: Days 3-7
Exam completed: Days 7-14
Medical records requested: Days 7-14
Underwriting decision: Days 21-45
Policy issued: Days 30-60
```

**Accelerated Underwriting:**
- No medical exam for certain applicants
- Based on data analytics, prescription drug history, credit
- Faster approval (days instead of weeks)
- Lower coverage amounts typically ($500,000-$1,000,000)

### Health Conditions and Underwriting

**Well-Controlled Conditions (May Get Preferred or Standard):**
- High blood pressure (controlled with medication)
- High cholesterol (controlled)
- Thyroid disorder (controlled)
- Asthma (mild, well-controlled)

**Moderate Conditions (Standard to Table Rated):**
- Diabetes (Type 1 or 2, controlled)
- Sleep apnea
- Obesity (BMI 30-40)
- Previous cancer (in remission 5+ years)

**Severe Conditions (Table Rated or Declined):**
- Recent heart attack or stroke
- Active cancer
- Severe diabetes (uncontrolled, complications)
- Severe obesity (BMI 40+)
- Drug/alcohol abuse

**Example:**
```
Applicant: Age 45, male, Type 2 diabetes diagnosed 3 years ago

Medical Exam Results:
- HbA1c: 6.8% (good control)
- Blood pressure: 130/80 (good)
- Cholesterol: 180 (good)
- Weight: Slightly overweight but not obese
- Medications: Metformin only
- No complications

Underwriting Decision: Standard or Table 2 (25-50% premium increase)

Premium for $1M, 20-year term:
- Preferred: $1,200/year
- Standard: $2,000/year
- Table 2: $2,500/year

Rationale: Diabetes increases mortality risk, but good control mitigates. Table rated but insurable.
```

## Key Riders and Features

### Waiver of Premium

**Benefit:** Waives premiums if insured becomes disabled

**Requirements:**
- Typically 6-month waiting period
- Total disability (unable to work in own occupation or any occupation)
- Continues until recovery or age 65

**Cost:** Typically 5-10% of base premium

**Example:**
```
Policy: $1M, annual premium $1,200
Rider cost: $120/year

If disabled at age 50:
- Premiums waived from age 50 to recovery or age 65
- Savings: $1,200/year × 15 years = $18,000
- Coverage remains in force
```

### Accelerated Death Benefit (ADB)

**Benefit:** Access death benefit if terminally ill

**Trigger:**
- Terminal illness (life expectancy 6-24 months depending on policy)
- Chronic illness (unable to perform 2+ activities of daily living)

**Amount:** Typically 25-100% of death benefit

**Cost:** Often included at no charge or minimal cost

**Use:** Pay for end-of-life care, medical expenses, final wishes

**Example:**
```
Policy: $500,000 death benefit
Diagnosis: Terminal cancer, 12 months to live

ADB: Access $400,000 of death benefit while living
Remaining death benefit: $100,000 to beneficiaries
```

### Long-Term Care (LTC) Rider

**Benefit:** Use death benefit for long-term care expenses

**Hybrid Life/LTC Policy:**
- Pay LTC benefits if need care
- Death benefit to heirs if don't need care
- Solves "use it or lose it" problem of standalone LTC

**Triggers:** Unable to perform 2+ ADLs or cognitive impairment

**Monthly Benefit:** Typically 2-4% of death benefit per month

**Example:**
```
Policy: $500,000 death benefit with LTC rider

Monthly LTC benefit: 2% = $10,000/month
Total LTC pool: $500,000

If need LTC:
- Receive $10,000/month for up to 50 months
- If use $300,000 for care, remaining $200,000 death benefit to heirs

If don't need LTC:
- Full $500,000 death benefit to heirs
```

### Return of Premium (ROP)

**Benefit:** Returns all premiums paid if outlive term

**Cost:** 50-100%+ higher than standard term

**Example:**
```
Age 40, $1M, 20-year term

Standard term: $600/year
ROP term: $1,200/year

If outlive 20 years:
- Total premiums paid: $24,000
- Return of premium: $24,000
- Net cost: $0

If die during term:
- Death benefit: $1,000,000
- No premium return (beneficiaries get death benefit)
```

**Analysis:**
- Premium difference: $600/year
- If invest $600/year at 5% for 20 years: $20,500
- Better to buy standard term and invest difference (typically)

### Guaranteed Insurability

**Benefit:** Buy additional coverage without medical exam

**Trigger Events:**
- Marriage
- Birth of child
- Home purchase
- Certain ages (25, 30, 35, 40, etc.)

**Limitations:**
- Maximum increase per event (typically $25,000-$50,000)
- Must exercise within timeframe (90 days of event)

**Cost:** Typically 5-15% of base premium

**Best For:** Young clients expecting life changes

## Tax Treatment of Life Insurance

### Death Benefit

**Generally Tax-Free:**
- Proceeds to beneficiary are income tax-free (IRC § 101)
- No income tax on $1M death benefit

**Exceptions:**
- Transfer for value rule (if policy sold)
- Employer-owned life insurance (some cases)

### Cash Value Growth

**Tax-Deferred:**
- Cash value grows without annual taxation
- No 1099 on annual gains

**Withdrawals:**
- Return of basis (premiums paid) first: Tax-free
- Gains above basis: Taxable as ordinary income

**Example:**
```
Whole life policy:
- Total premiums paid: $100,000
- Current cash value: $150,000

Withdraw $100,000:
- Tax-free (return of basis)

Withdraw $120,000:
- $100,000 tax-free (basis)
- $20,000 taxable (gain above basis)
```

### Loans

**Tax-Free:**
- Policy loans not taxable
- Interest accrues
- Reduces death benefit if not repaid

**Modified Endowment Contract (MEC):**
- If policy fails 7-pay test (overfunded)
- Loses tax-free loan treatment
- Loans taxable (LIFO - gains first)
- 10% early withdrawal penalty if under 59½

### Estate Tax

**Included in Estate if:**
- Insured owns policy
- Insured has incidents of ownership (can change beneficiary, borrow, etc.)

**Excluded from Estate if:**
- Owned by Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (ILIT)
- Owned by spouse or third party (no incidents of ownership)

**Example:**
```
Estate: $15 million
Life insurance: $5 million (owned by insured)
Total estate: $20 million

Estate tax exemption (2025): $13.99 million
Taxable estate: $6.01 million
Estate tax (40%): $2.4 million

Solution: Transfer policy to ILIT
- Life insurance excluded from estate
- Gross estate: $15 million
- Estate tax: $400,000 (($15M - $13.99M) × 40%)
- **Savings: $2 million**
```

## Recommendations by Client Situation

**Young Family (Ages 30-40, Children, Mortgage):**
- **Need:** High coverage, temporary
- **Product:** 20 or 30-year level term
- **Amount:** 10-15× income or needs-based analysis
- **Cost:** Most affordable for large coverage

**Mid-Career (Ages 40-55, Accumulating Wealth):**
- **Need:** Coverage plus potential cash value
- **Product:** Term + permanent (ladder approach)
  - Term for temporary needs
  - Whole life or IUL for permanent
- **Amount:** Decreasing need as assets accumulate

**High Net Worth (Estate Planning):**
- **Need:** Estate liquidity, wealth transfer
- **Product:** Whole life or guaranteed UL in ILIT
- **Amount:** Sufficient to pay estate taxes
- **Strategy:** Irrevocable trust ownership

**Business Owner (Key Person, Buy-Sell):**
- **Need:** Business protection
- **Product:** Term or permanent depending on need
- **Amount:** Based on business value or income replacement
- **Structure:** Business owns and pays premiums

**Retirement Age (Ages 60-70):**
- **Need:** Final expenses, legacy
- **Product:** Whole life or guaranteed UL
- **Amount:** $50,000-$250,000
- **Note:** Term very expensive at this age

---

**Life insurance planning requires thorough needs analysis, product knowledge, and understanding of underwriting. Match product to client's specific needs, budget, and time horizon. Always disclose all fees, charges, and surrender periods. Ensure suitability based on client's financial situation and objectives.**
