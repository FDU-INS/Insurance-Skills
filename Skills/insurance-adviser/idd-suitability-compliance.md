# IDD Suitability and Compliance Guide

Comprehensive guide to Insurance Distribution Directive (IDD) suitability requirements and compliance for insurance-based investment products (IBIPs).

## IDD Suitability Framework

### When Suitability Required

**Suitability Assessment Required For:**
- Insurance-based investment products (IBIPs)
- When providing advice

**Insurance-Based Investment Products (IBIPs):**
- Life insurance with investment component exposed to market fluctuations
- Examples:
  - Unit-linked life insurance
  - Index-linked annuities
  - With-profits policies
  - Variable annuities

**NOT IBIPs (no suitability required):**
- Pure protection (term life, whole life with guaranteed returns)
- Health insurance
- Property/casualty insurance
- Fixed annuities with guaranteed returns

### Suitability vs Appropriateness

**Suitability (Advice on IBIPs):**
- Comprehensive assessment
- Knowledge & experience + financial situation + objectives
- Must explain why suitable
- Suitability statement required

**Appropriateness (Execution-Only on IBIPs):**
- Limited assessment
- Knowledge & experience only
- Warn if not appropriate
- Can proceed if customer insists

**No Assessment (Non-Advice, Non-Complex Products):**
- Pure protection policies
- Simple products
- Customer initiative, no advice

## Information to Obtain for Suitability

### IDD Article 30 Requirements

**Three Categories of Information:**

**1. Knowledge and Experience:**
- Types of insurance products familiar with
- Previous insurance purchases
- Education level
- Profession
- Investment knowledge (for IBIPs)
- Understanding of risk and returns

**2. Financial Situation:**
- Income (employment, business, pensions, other)
- Regular financial commitments
- Assets (savings, investments, property)
- Liabilities (mortgages, loans, debts)
- Dependents
- Existing insurance coverage

**3. Investment Objectives and Insurance Needs:**
- Insurance need (protection, savings, retirement income)
- Investment horizon (short, medium, long-term)
- Risk tolerance (low, medium, high)
- Preference for capital protection or growth
- Need for liquidity or access to funds
- Sustainability preferences (ESG considerations)

### Detailed Information Gathering Example

**Client: Age 40, Married, 2 Children**

**Knowledge & Experience:**
```
Q: Have you previously purchased investment products?
A: Yes, mutual funds through bank

Q: How familiar are you with unit-linked life insurance?
A: Limited - understand concept but never purchased

Q: Do you understand that investment returns can go up and down?
A: Yes, accept this risk

Q: Education and occupation?
A: University degree, software engineer

Assessment: Informed investor, familiar with investment concepts, suitable for IBIP with explanation
```

**Financial Situation:**
```
Income:
- Employment: €80,000/year
- Spouse employment: €45,000/year
- Total: €125,000/year

Assets:
- Savings: €30,000
- Investments: €50,000 (mutual funds)
- Home: €400,000 (€250,000 mortgage)

Liabilities:
- Mortgage: €250,000 (€1,500/month)
- Car loan: €15,000 (€400/month)

Monthly expenses:
- Mortgage: €1,500
- Car: €400
- Living expenses: €3,000
- Insurance: €300
- Total: €5,200/month

Surplus: €125,000/12 = €10,417/month - €5,200 = €5,217/month

Assessment: Strong financial position, can afford investment and bear potential losses
```

**Insurance Needs & Objectives:**
```
Q: What is your primary insurance need?
A: Family protection if I die, plus build long-term savings for retirement

Q: How much protection do you need?
A: Enough to pay off mortgage (€250,000) + provide income for family + children's education
Estimated: €600,000

Q: Investment time horizon?
A: 25 years until retirement at age 65

Q: Risk tolerance?
A: Medium - willing to accept volatility for growth, but not aggressive

Q: Need for liquidity?
A: Some access in emergency, but primarily long-term

Q: Sustainability preferences?
A: Prefer investments that consider environmental and social factors

Assessment: Suitable for unit-linked life insurance with €600,000 sum assured, invested in balanced ESG funds, 25-year horizon
```

## Suitability Assessment Process

### Step 1: Information Collection

**Methods:**
- Face-to-face interview
- Questionnaire (paper or digital)
- Telephone discussion
- Combination

**Best Practice:**
- Interactive discussion (not just form-filling)
- Verify information (don't rely solely on customer statements)
- Update regularly (at least annually or when material change)

### Step 2: Analysis

**Assess Product Match:**
- Does product meet customer's insurance need?
- Is investment component suitable for financial situation?
- Does risk level match tolerance?
- Is time horizon appropriate?
- Are costs reasonable for customer's budget?

**Red Flags (Product NOT Suitable):**
- Customer cannot afford premiums
- Time horizon too short (product designed for long-term)
- Risk level exceeds tolerance (aggressive product for conservative investor)
- Customer doesn't understand product despite explanation
- Surrender charges problematic given liquidity needs

### Step 3: Suitability Statement

**IDD Requirement:** Provide suitability statement before contract conclusion

**Contents:**
- Recommendation (which product)
- Why suitable (reference to customer information)
- How it meets objectives
- Financial situation compatibility
- Knowledge/experience assessment
- Target market disclosure (if outside target market)
- Warnings (risks, costs, surrender charges)

**Example Suitability Statement:**

```
SUITABILITY STATEMENT
Insurance-Based Investment Product Recommendation

Customer: John Smith, DOB 15/03/1984
Date: 1 March 2025
Adviser: Mary O'Brien

RECOMMENDATION:
Unit-Linked Life Insurance Policy
- Provider: ABC Life Insurance
- Sum Assured: €600,000
- Premium: €400/month (€4,800/year)
- Investment Option: Balanced ESG Fund
- Policy Term: 25 years (to age 65)

WHY THIS IS SUITABLE FOR YOU:

Insurance Need:
You have a need for life insurance to protect your family in the event of your premature death. You indicated you want to cover your mortgage (€250,000), provide income for your family, and fund children's education. The sum assured of €600,000 meets this need.

Investment Objective:
You have a long-term investment horizon (25 years to retirement) and seek growth with moderate risk. The Balanced ESG Fund invests 60% in equities and 40% in bonds, aligned with your medium risk tolerance. Your sustainability preferences are met through the ESG fund selection.

Financial Situation:
Your combined household income is €125,000/year with monthly surplus of €5,217 after expenses. The €400/month premium is affordable (7.7% of surplus, 3.8% of gross income). You have €80,000 in savings/investments, providing a financial cushion. You can bear potential investment losses given your assets and income stability.

Knowledge and Experience:
You have a university education, professional occupation, and previous experience with investment products (mutual funds). You understand investment risk and that returns can fluctuate. While your knowledge of unit-linked insurance is limited, the product structure and risks have been explained, and you have demonstrated understanding.

TARGET MARKET:
This product's target market is:
- Medium to long-term investment horizon (10+ years)
- Medium risk tolerance
- Able to bear capital loss
- Need for life insurance protection
- Informed investors

You are within the target market for this product.

IMPORTANT WARNINGS:

Costs:
- Premium allocation charge: 5% of premiums in year 1, reducing to 0% by year 5
- Fund management charge: 0.75% annually
- Policy fee: €5/month
- Total cost year 1: Approximately 6% of premium
- Total cost year 10+: Approximately 1.5% of premium

Surrender Charges:
- Years 1-5: 5% of policy value (declining to 0% by year 6)
- Early surrender will result in significant costs and potential capital loss

Investment Risk:
- Policy value will fluctuate with investment performance
- You may receive back less than you paid in, especially if surrender early
- Not capital protected or guaranteed

Liquidity:
- Policy designed for long-term (25 years)
- Early surrender costly due to charges
- Partial withdrawals available but reduce sum assured and may trigger charges

If you have any questions or do not understand any aspect of this recommendation, please ask before proceeding. You should only proceed if you are comfortable with the recommendation and understand the risks.

I confirm that I have explained this recommendation to you and you have had opportunity to ask questions.

Customer Signature: _________________ Date: _______
Adviser Signature: _________________ Date: _______
```

### Step 4: Ongoing Suitability

**Periodic Review:**
- At least every 2 years (IDD requirement)
- Or more frequently if:
  - Material change in customer circumstances
  - Significant market event
  - Product change

**Review Process:**
1. Request updated customer information
2. Assess if product still suitable
3. Recommend changes if needed (switch funds, increase/decrease premium, etc.)
4. Document review and any actions

## Product Governance and Target Market

### Manufacturer Target Market (from Product Governance)

**Received from Insurer:**
- Target market description
- Positive target market (who product is for)
- Negative target market (who product is NOT for)

**Distributor Responsibility:**
- Understand target market
- Only distribute to compatible customers
- Identify own target market (may be subset)

### Suitability and Target Market Alignment

**If Customer Within Target Market:**
- Proceed with suitability assessment
- Product presumed appropriate (subject to individual assessment)

**If Customer Outside Target Market:**
- Can still recommend IF suitable for individual customer's circumstances
- Must provide enhanced explanation
- Document reasons why suitable despite target market mismatch
- Warn customer

**Example:**
```
Product: Aggressive Growth Unit-Linked Policy
Manufacturer Target Market: High risk tolerance, long horizon (15+ years), ages 25-50

Customer: Age 58, medium risk tolerance, 7-year horizon
- Outside target market (age, risk, horizon)

Suitability Assessment:
- Customer has significant wealth (€2 million net worth)
- This policy is 5% of total portfolio (€100,000)
- Remainder in conservative investments
- Customer experienced investor, understands risk
- Willing to accept volatility on small portion of portfolio

Conclusion: Suitable despite outside target market
- Overall portfolio allocation is conservative
- Customer can afford loss on this small allocation
- Documented rationale and customer understanding

Warning Provided:
"This product's target market is younger investors with high risk tolerance and longer time horizons. You are outside this target market due to your age (58) and investment horizon (7 years). However, given this represents only 5% of your overall portfolio and you have expressed willingness to accept higher risk on this portion, the product may still be suitable for you. Please confirm you understand this is outside the typical target market and accept the associated risks."
```

## Conflicts of Interest Disclosure

### IDD Article 28: Conflicts and Inducements

**Conflicts to Disclose:**

**1. Remuneration Structure:**
- Commission-based vs fee-based
- If commission: How calculated
- If varies by product: Disclose this creates conflict

**2. Proprietary Products:**
- Own company's products vs third-party
- Financial interest in recommending own products

**3. Incentives and Bonuses:**
- Sales targets
- Higher commission for certain products
- Non-monetary benefits

### Disclosure Examples

**Example 1: Commission-Based Adviser**
```
REMUNERATION DISCLOSURE

I am remunerated through commissions paid by insurance companies.

The commission I receive for this unit-linked policy is 3% of annual premiums for the first 5 years (€4,800/year × 3% × 5 years = €720).

This creates a conflict of interest as I have a financial incentive to recommend products and accept your application.

However, my recommendation is based on suitability for your needs and objectives, not on my compensation. I am required by IDD regulations to act in your best interest.

If you have concerns about this conflict, you may choose to:
- Seek a second opinion from another adviser
- Choose a fee-based adviser (pays no commission but charges direct fee)
- Ask me any questions about how commission affects my recommendation
```

**Example 2: Proprietary Product Conflict**
```
CONFLICT OF INTEREST DISCLOSURE

The unit-linked policy I am recommending is manufactured by XYZ Life Insurance, which is part of the same corporate group as my employer (ABC Distribution).

I receive standard commission (3% of premiums), but my employer benefits from the overall profitability of XYZ Life Insurance products.

This creates a conflict of interest as there is a financial incentive for our group to recommend XYZ products over competitors' products.

However, I have assessed third-party alternatives and this XYZ product is suitable for your needs. I am required to make recommendations based on suitability, not on corporate relationships.

Third-party alternatives considered:
- DEF Life Insurance Unit-Linked Policy (similar features, slightly higher costs)
- GHI Insurance Investment Bond (different structure, not life insurance)

I recommend the XYZ product because: [specific reasons related to customer's situation]

You are free to purchase a competing product if you prefer. I can provide information on alternatives upon request.
```

## Common Deficiencies and Best Practices

### Common IDD Suitability Deficiencies

**1. Insufficient Information Gathering:**
- Generic questions, not tailored
- Failing to probe or verify
- Accepting customer statements without inquiry
- Solution: Comprehensive questionnaire, follow-up questions, verification

**2. Inadequate Suitability Statement:**
- Boilerplate language, not personalized
- Doesn't explain WHY suitable
- Missing target market disclosure
- Solution: Detailed, customized statements referencing specific customer information

**3. Recommending Outside Target Market Without Justification:**
- Selling to customers outside target market
- No documented rationale
- No enhanced warnings
- Solution: Only recommend outside target market if genuinely suitable, document extensively

**4. Failing to Update Information:**
- No periodic reviews
- Stale customer information
- Changed circumstances not captured
- Solution: Annual reviews minimum, update when life events occur

**5. Conflicts Not Disclosed:**
- Commission structure not explained
- Proprietary product conflict not disclosed
- Incentive arrangements not mentioned
- Solution: Comprehensive conflict disclosure upfront

### Best Practices

**1. Robust Information Gathering:**
- Use standardized questionnaire (ensures consistency)
- Interactive discussion (not just form)
- Probe for understanding (ensure customer comprehends)
- Document everything (notes from discussion)
- Verify where possible (review documents, not just rely on statements)

**2. Personalized Suitability Statements:**
- Reference specific customer information
- Explain product features relevant to customer needs
- Clear "why suitable" section
- Prominent risk warnings
- Plain language (not jargon)

**3. Target Market Compliance:**
- Understand manufacturer's target market
- Match customers to appropriate products
- Exceptional sales outside target market require enhanced documentation
- Monitor sales by target market (quality assurance)

**4. Transparent Conflict Disclosure:**
- Disclose upfront (before recommendation)
- Clear, prominent language
- Explain how conflicts managed
- Offer alternatives

**5. Ongoing Review:**
- Contact customers at least every 2 years
- Update information
- Reassess suitability
- Document review

**6. Training and Competence:**
- Ensure advisers understand IDD suitability requirements
- Product knowledge (features, risks, costs, target market)
- Suitability assessment skills
- Regular training and updates

**7. Compliance Oversight:**
- Quality assurance reviews (sample suitability files)
- Monitor for deficiencies
- Remediate issues promptly
- Management information (suitability metrics)

## Documentation and Recordkeeping

### Required Documentation

**Customer File Must Contain:**
- [ ] Suitability questionnaire (completed, dated, signed)
- [ ] Suitability statement (provided before contract, customer copy)
- [ ] Product information provided (IPID, KID, policy terms)
- [ ] Target market information (from manufacturer)
- [ ] Conflict disclosures (commission, proprietary products, etc.)
- [ ] Customer instructions or preferences
- [ ] Periodic review documentation (if ongoing relationship)
- [ ] Correspondence (emails, letters, notes from meetings)
- [ ] Complaints (if any) and resolution

**Retention Period:**
- Varies by member state (typically 5-10 years after policy ends)
- Check local NCA requirements

### Quality Assurance File Review

**Sample Reviews:**
- Select random sample (e.g., 10% of files quarterly)
- Review for compliance with IDD suitability requirements

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Information obtained adequate and verified?
- [ ] Suitability statement provided before contract?
- [ ] Statement explains WHY suitable with reference to customer info?
- [ ] Product within target market or exception documented?
- [ ] Conflicts disclosed adequately?
- [ ] Customer signed/acknowledged understanding?
- [ ] Periodic review conducted (if applicable)?
- [ ] File documentation complete?

**Deficiency Handling:**
- Identify root cause (training gap, process issue, individual performance)
- Remediate (retrain, update procedures, individual coaching)
- Contact customer if material deficiency (offer to review, remediate if mis-sold)
- Report to compliance/management

## NCA Inspections and Examinations

### Preparation for Inspection

**Before NCA Inspection:**
1. Conduct internal file review (identify and remediate deficiencies)
2. Ensure all files complete and organized
3. Review policies and procedures (up to date with IDD requirements)
4. Train staff on inspection process
5. Designate liaison person

**Common NCA Focus Areas:**
- Suitability assessment quality (adequate information, personalized statements)
- Sales outside target market (frequency, justification)
- Conflicts disclosure (adequate, prominent)
- Periodic reviews (conducted, documented)
- Training and competence (staff qualifications, ongoing training)

### During Inspection

**Cooperation:**
- Provide documents requested promptly
- Answer questions truthfully and concisely
- Don't volunteer beyond what's asked
- Take notes on discussions

**Sample File Review:**
- NCA typically reviews sample of customer files
- Selects cross-section (products, advisers, time periods)
- Reviews for compliance

### Post-Inspection

**Findings:**
- NCA issues report (typically within 2-3 months)
- Identifies deficiencies, breaches, recommendations

**Response:**
- Acknowledge findings
- Provide action plan (how deficiencies will be remediated)
- Implement changes
- Follow-up evidence of implementation

**Example Action Plan:**
```
Deficiency: Suitability statements do not adequately explain why recommended products are suitable

Root Cause:
- Template-based statements with insufficient customization
- Advisers not trained on detailed statement requirements
- No quality review process

Remediation:
1. Updated suitability statement template (includes prompts for customization)
2. Training for all advisers (completed 15 April 2025, attendance records attached)
3. Quality review process implemented (compliance reviews 25% of files monthly)
4. Reviewed all suitability statements from past 12 months, contacted customers where deficiencies identified

Implementation Date: 30 April 2025
Responsible: Compliance Officer
Evidence: Training materials, updated template, quality review reports (attached)
```
