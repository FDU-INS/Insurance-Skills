# Protection Policy Trust Templates and Explanations

## Standard Provider Trusts

Most insurance providers supply standard trust templates free of charge. These are suitable for the majority of cases and do not require solicitor involvement.

### When to Use Provider Trusts

**Suitable For**:
- Standard family situations
- Straightforward beneficiary arrangements
- Simple estates
- Cost-conscious clients
- Quick setup required

**When Solicitor Trust May Be Better**:
- Complex family situations (multiple marriages, estranged children)
- Very high value estates (£2m+)
- Specific tax planning needs
- Vulnerable beneficiaries (disabled, minors with special needs)
- Business protection with complex ownership

## Discretionary Trust Template Structure

### 1. Parties

**Life Assured(s)**: Person(s) whose life is insured

**Settlor**: Person creating the trust (usually life assured)

**Trustees**: People who will manage the trust
- Minimum 2 recommended
- Maximum 4 typical
- Should include someone younger than life assured
- Trustworthy and capable

**Beneficiaries**: Class of people who may benefit
- Usually "spouse and children"
- Can be wider ("all relatives")
- Can exclude specific individuals

### 2. Trust Property

**What's Held in Trust**:
- The life insurance policy
- All proceeds and benefits
- Any bonuses or additional benefits

### 3. Trustee Powers

**Powers Typically Included**:
- Power to apply proceeds for beneficiaries
- Power to invest proceeds (if not immediately distributed)
- Power to add or remove beneficiaries (within class)
- Power to appoint new trustees
- Power to obtain professional advice
- Power to pay expenses

### 4. Beneficiary Class

**Standard Wording**:
> "The beneficiaries shall be the settlor's spouse and children and remoter issue"

**Meaning**:
- Spouse: Current legal spouse
- Children: All children (including adopted)
- Remoter issue: Grandchildren, great-grandchildren

**Alternative Wording** (Children Only):
> "The beneficiaries shall be all children of the settlor (including adopted children) and their remoter issue"

**Alternative Wording** (Excluding Specific Person)**:
> "The beneficiaries shall be the settlor's spouse and all children (except [name]) and their remoter issue"

### 5. Distribution Provisions

**Discretionary**:
- Trustees decide who gets what
- Can distribute equally or unequally
- Can distribute immediately or hold in trust
- Flexibility for changing circumstances

**Example Wording**:
> "The trustees shall have absolute discretion to pay or apply the whole or any part of the trust fund to or for the benefit of all or such one or more of the beneficiaries as they think fit"

### 6. Trust Period

**Typical Period**: 125 years (max under Perpetuities and Accumulations Act)

**Purpose**: Allows flexibility for long-term holding if beneficial

### 7. Governing Law

**Standard**: Law of England and Wales (or Scotland/Northern Ireland if appropriate)

## Sample Letter of Wishes

**Purpose**: Non-binding guidance to trustees

### Template Letter of Wishes

```
LETTER OF WISHES

To: The Trustees of the [Provider Name] Discretionary Trust dated [date]

Policy Number: [number]

Life Assured: [name]

Dear Trustees,

This letter is to provide you with guidance on how I would wish the proceeds of the above life insurance policy to be distributed in the event of a claim. While I understand this letter is not legally binding, I hope you will give serious consideration to my wishes.

1. PRIMARY BENEFICIARY

I would wish the trustees to ensure that [spouse name], my [husband/wife], is provided for as their first priority. Specifically:

- [Spouse name] should receive sufficient funds to:
  * Maintain our family home (currently valued at approximately £[amount])
  * Clear the outstanding mortgage (currently £[amount])
  * Meet living expenses for the foreseeable future
  * Ensure financial security and independence

2. CHILDREN'S PROVISION

Subject to [spouse name]'s needs being met, I would wish any remaining funds to be distributed to my children:

- [Child 1 name], currently aged [age]
- [Child 2 name], currently aged [age]

I would suggest the following approach for my children:

- Funds to be held in trust until each child reaches age [25]
- Trustees may release funds earlier for:
  * Education costs (university fees, training)
  * First home deposit
  * Starting a business
  * Medical or emergency needs
- Equal distribution between children unless circumstances warrant otherwise

3. SPECIAL CIRCUMSTANCES

[Include any specific wishes, for example:]

- If [spouse name] remarries, I would wish the trustees to ensure that a fair portion is preserved for my children
- [Child name] has special needs and may require ongoing financial support beyond age 25
- I would wish some provision to be made for [other person, e.g., elderly parent] if funds allow

4. TIMING

I would generally wish for:
- Immediate distribution to [spouse name] for urgent needs (funeral costs, mortgage payments, living expenses)
- Distribution to children to be made in stages or held until reaching specified age

5. INVESTMENT

If funds are held for any period, I would wish the trustees to invest prudently, balancing growth with security, appropriate to the likely time period before distribution.

6. TRUSTEES' DISCRETION

I recognize that circumstances may change and the situation at the time of my death may be very different from now. I therefore trust the trustees to use their discretion in the best interests of the beneficiaries, taking into account all relevant circumstances at that time.

This letter reflects my wishes as of [date]. I will review and update it periodically as circumstances change.

Signed: _______________________

Date: _______________________

Witnessed by: _______________________

Date: _______________________
```

## Absolute Trust Template Structure

### Simpler Than Discretionary

**Key Differences**:
- Fixed beneficiary (named individual)
- No trustee discretion
- Beneficiary has immediate right to proceeds
- Less flexible

### Standard Wording (Absolute to Spouse)

**Beneficiary Clause**:
> "The trustees shall hold the trust fund for [spouse name] absolutely"

**Meaning**: All proceeds paid to spouse, no discretion

### Standard Wording (Absolute to Children Equally)

**Beneficiary Clause**:
> "The trustees shall hold the trust fund for the children of the settlor who are living at the date of the claim in equal shares absolutely, and if any child has predeceased leaving children, such children shall take their parent's share equally"

**Meaning**: Equal split among living children (grandchildren take deceased child's share)

## Survivor's Discretionary Trust

### For Joint Life First Death Policies

**Special Features**:
- Survivor becomes trustee automatically
- Survivor has discretion over distribution
- Protects children's interests while giving survivor control

### Key Clauses

**On First Death**:
- Surviving life assured becomes trustee
- Other original trustees continue
- Survivor gains control

**Beneficiaries**:
- Survivor
- Children
- Remoter issue

**Distribution**:
- Survivor decides how to distribute
- Can provide for themselves and children as needed
- Flexible for changing circumstances

### Sample Wording

> "Upon the first death, the surviving life assured shall become a trustee and together with the other trustees shall have absolute discretion to distribute the trust fund among the beneficiaries (being the surviving life assured, the children and remoter issue of the lives assured) in such shares and at such times as they think fit"

## Split Trust Template

### For Combined Life and Critical Illness

**Structure**:
- Life insurance benefit to one beneficiary (usually spouse)
- Critical illness benefit to policyholder
- Separates death and illness benefits

### Key Provisions

**Life Insurance Benefit**:
> "Upon the death of the life assured, the trustees shall hold the life insurance benefit for [spouse name] absolutely"

**Critical Illness Benefit**:
> "Upon a critical illness claim by the life assured during their lifetime, the critical illness benefit shall be paid to the life assured absolutely"

**Rationale**:
- Policyholder keeps CI benefit for their own use
- Spouse protected on death
- Avoids gifting CI benefit away

## Trust Registration Service (TRS) Information

### When to Register

**Must Register If**:
- Trust generates income or gains before payout
- Trust holds funds for period of time
- Trustees make discretionary distributions over time

**Usually Don't Need to Register**:
- Pure protection trust
- Proceeds paid out quickly after claim
- No income or gains generated

### Registration Timeline

**Deadline**:
- Within 90 days of trust becoming liable to UK tax
- Or by 31 January following creation if earlier
- Or before making distribution

### Information Needed for Registration

- Trust details (date, type)
- Settlor details (name, address, DOB, NI)
- Trustee details (names, addresses, DOBs)
- Beneficiary details (names or class description)
- Asset details (policy number, provider, sum assured)

### HMRC Portal

**Access**: HMRC's Trust Registration Service online portal

**Responsibility**: Trustees must register (not adviser)

## Practical Trust Setup Process

### Step 1: Choose Trust Type

**Decision Factors**:
- Family circumstances (simple or complex?)
- Need for flexibility (discretionary) or certainty (absolute)?
- Number and ages of beneficiaries
- Tax planning needs

### Step 2: Choose Trustees

**Recommendations**:
- Minimum 2 (1 if sole client)
- Maximum 4
- Include spouse (if appropriate)
- Include younger person (to outlive you)
- Consider professional trustee for complex situations

**Avoid**:
- Only trustees older than you (may predecease)
- Only 1 trustee (risk if they die or become incapable)
- Untrustworthy or irresponsible individuals

### Step 3: Complete Trust Deed

**Process**:
1. Obtain provider's standard trust form (usually free)
2. Complete all sections
3. Insert names of trustees and beneficiaries
4. Sign in presence of independent witness
5. Witness signs
6. Date all signatures

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**:
- Forgetting to date
- Life assured witnessing own signature (invalid)
- Spouse witnessing (should be independent)
- Incomplete beneficiary details
- Not keeping copy for records

### Step 4: Send to Provider

**What to Send**:
- Completed original trust deed
- Covering letter with policy number
- Keep photocopy for your records

**Provider Action**:
- Notes trust on policy
- Updates beneficiary details
- Sends acknowledgment
- May send copy back stamped

### Step 5: Letter of Wishes (Optional but Recommended)

**Create**:
- Separate document
- Not sent to provider
- Kept with trust deed
- Given to trustees

**Update**:
- Every 3-5 years
- After major life changes
- Keep dated versions

### Step 6: Record Keeping

**Keep Safe**:
- Original trust deed (trustees)
- Copy for life assured
- Copy for adviser file
- Letter of wishes (separate, for trustees)

**Tell Trustees**:
- That trust exists
- Where deed is kept
- Policy details
- How to claim

## Common Trust Scenarios with Solutions

### Scenario 1: Married, Young Children

**Situation**:
- Married couple
- Children aged 3 and 6
- £300,000 joint life first death policy

**Recommended Trust**: Survivor's Discretionary Trust

**Beneficiaries**: Surviving spouse and children

**Trustees**: Both spouses + trusted relative/friend

**Letter of Wishes**:
- Survivor to receive funds for immediate needs
- Balance for children held until age 25
- Trustees (including survivor) have discretion

### Scenario 2: Second Marriage, Children from First Marriage

**Situation**:
- Remarried
- Children from first marriage aged 18, 20
- Current spouse
- £250,000 life insurance

**Recommended Trust**: Discretionary Trust

**Beneficiaries**: All children (not current spouse as main beneficiary)

**Trustees**: Adult child from first marriage + trusted friend (NOT current spouse)

**Letter of Wishes**:
- Provide for current spouse's reasonable needs
- But ensure children from first marriage inherit majority
- Specific amounts suggested

**Rationale**: Protects children's inheritance while providing for spouse

### Scenario 3: Single Person, No Dependents

**Situation**:
- Single, divorced
- Children are adults and financially independent
- £100,000 life insurance for funeral and legacy

**Recommended Trust**: Absolute Trust (if equal shares desired) or Discretionary

**Beneficiaries**: Children equally

**Trustees**: The children themselves (or two of them)

**Rationale**:
- Simple structure
- Avoids probate
- Equal treatment of children

### Scenario 4: Business Partners

**Situation**:
- 3 business partners, equal shareholdings
- Shareholder protection insurance
- £500,000 per partner

**Recommended Trust**: Absolute Trust

**Beneficiaries**: Other two partners (in proportion to their shareholdings)

**Trustees**: All three partners

**Additional**: Cross-option agreement in place

**Rationale**:
- Business needs certainty
- Proceeds must go to partners to buy shares
- Aligned with legal agreements

## Trust Amendment and Revocation

### Amending Trustees

**When Needed**:
- Trustee dies
- Trustee becomes incapable
- Want to add additional trustee
- Trustee moves abroad

**Process**:
1. Deed of Appointment and/or Retirement
2. Sign and witness
3. Notify provider
4. Update records

### Changing Beneficiaries

**Discretionary Trust**:
- Trustees can add/remove within permitted class
- Requires deed of appointment
- Must be within trust powers

**Absolute Trust**:
- Cannot change beneficiary
- Fixed at creation

### Revoking Trust

**When Possible**:
- Some trusts allow revocation
- Requires all parties' consent (settlor, trustees, beneficiaries)
- Complex process

**Usually Better**:
- Take out new policy with correct trust
- Surrender old policy

**Professional Advice Needed**: Solicitor essential

## Best Practice Summary

**Always**:
- Recommend trust for all protection policies
- Use provider trust (free and suitable for most)
- Ensure minimum 2 trustees
- Create letter of wishes (discretionary trusts)
- Keep good records
- Set review date
- Explain TRS requirements

**Never**:
- Recommend no trust without clear reason
- Use solicitor trust unless necessary (extra cost)
- Appoint only 1 trustee
- Forget to send to provider
- Forget to give trustees a copy
- Leave for years without review

**Review Triggers**:
- Marriage/divorce
- Birth of child
- Death of beneficiary or trustee
- Major family change
- Every 3-5 years minimum
