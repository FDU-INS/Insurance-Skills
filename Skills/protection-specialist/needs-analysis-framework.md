# Protection Needs Analysis Framework

## Purpose

Systematic approach to calculating protection needs ensuring clients have adequate cover for income replacement, debt coverage, and future financial commitments.

## Methods of Needs Analysis

### 1. Income Replacement Method

**Purpose**: Replace lost income for dependents

**Calculation**:
```
Annual Income × Number of Years Support Needed = Sum Assured
```

**Example**:
- Annual income: £50,000
- Years until youngest child independent: 15 years
- Basic calculation: £50,000 × 15 = £750,000

**Refinements**:
- Deduct existing savings/investments
- Deduct spouse's income (if applicable)
- Add inflation adjustment (2-3% compound)
- Consider State benefits (Bereavement Allowance)

**Typical Multiples**:
- Single income household: 10-15× salary
- Dual income household: 5-10× salary per person
- No dependents: Debt coverage only

### 2. Debt Coverage Method

**Purpose**: Repay all debts and liabilities

**Include**:
- Outstanding mortgage balance
- Personal loans
- Credit card balances
- Car finance
- Funeral costs (£3,000-£5,000 average)
- Final expenses (probate, legal fees)

**Example**:
- Mortgage: £180,000
- Personal loan: £8,000
- Car finance: £12,000
- Funeral costs: £4,000
- Total: £204,000

### 3. Human Life Value (HLV) Method

**Purpose**: Present value of future earnings

**Formula**:
```
Current Annual Income × Years to Retirement × Discount Factor
```

**Example**:
- Current income: £50,000
- Years to retirement: 25
- Discount rate: 3%
- HLV ≈ £875,000

**Use**: Sophisticated approach for high earners or business protection

### 4. Comprehensive Method (Recommended)

**Purpose**: Itemize all needs and costs

**Components**:

**Immediate Needs (Lump Sum)**:
- Funeral costs
- Outstanding debts (mortgage, loans)
- Emergency fund (6 months expenses)
- Legal and probate fees

**Ongoing Needs (Annual)**:
- Living expenses (reduce by deceased's personal spending)
- Childcare costs
- School fees / university costs
- Spouse's pension shortfall

**Future Costs (Lump Sum)**:
- University fees for children (£30,000-£50,000 per child)
- Wedding gifts (optional)
- Property maintenance reserves

**Less**:
- Existing life insurance
- Savings and investments (accessible)
- Spouse's income
- State benefits

**Example Comprehensive Analysis**:

## Protection Needs by Life Stage

### Young Single (No Dependents)
- **Priority**: Debt coverage, funeral costs
- **Typical Need**: £50,000-£100,000
- **Product**: Basic term assurance

### Married/Cohabiting (No Children)
- **Priority**: Mortgage, partner's income replacement
- **Typical Need**: £150,000-£300,000
- **Products**: Joint life first death, mortgage protection

### Young Family (Children Under 10)
- **Priority**: Income replacement, mortgage, future costs
- **Typical Need**: £300,000-£750,000
- **Products**: Level term, family income benefit, critical illness

### Established Family (Children 10-18)
- **Priority**: Income, education costs, mortgage
- **Typical Need**: £250,000-£600,000
- **Products**: Decreasing term (if mortgage reducing), level term

### Pre-Retirement (Children Independent)
- **Priority**: Debt coverage, spouse's pension
- **Typical Need**: £100,000-£200,000
- **Products**: Short-term cover, whole of life

### Retirement (65+)
- **Priority**: Funeral costs, IHT planning
- **Typical Need**: £10,000-£50,000+
- **Products**: Over-50s plans, whole of life, gift trust

## Critical Illness Needs Analysis

### Purpose
Provide lump sum if diagnosed with serious illness (cancer, heart attack, stroke, etc.)

### Typical Uses
- Mortgage repayment (reduce financial stress)
- Home adaptations (wheelchair access, stairlift)
- Private medical treatment
- Replacement income (if unable to work)
- Debt clearance
- Spouse reducing hours to care

### Calculating Sum Assured
**Option 1: Mortgage Balance**
- Match mortgage outstanding
- Clears largest debt if ill

**Option 2: Income Multiple**
- 3-5 years' income
- Provides financial cushion
- Example: £50,000 salary × 4 = £200,000

**Option 3: Comprehensive**
- Mortgage + adaptations + income replacement
- Most expensive but most comprehensive

### CI vs Life Insurance
- Often combined (life + CI on same policy)
- CI claim pays out and policy ends
- Cheaper than separate policies

## Income Protection Needs Analysis

### Purpose
Replace lost income if unable to work due to sickness or accident

### Calculating Benefit Amount

**Maximum Coverage**: Typically 50-70% of gross income

**Example**:
- Gross annual income: £50,000
- Maximum IP benefit: 60% = £30,000 per year (£2,500/month)

**Why Not 100%?**
- Prevents over-insurance
- Benefits usually tax-free (so 60% gross ≈ similar to net)
- Incentive to return to work

### Deferred Period Selection

**Deferred Period**: Waiting time before benefits start

**Options**:
- **1 week**: Expensive, rarely needed (most have sick pay)
- **4 weeks**: Common for self-employed
- **13 weeks**: Popular balance
- **26 weeks**: Lower cost, works with employer sick pay
- **52 weeks**: Cheapest, long-term protection only

**How to Choose**:
1. Check employer sick pay duration
2. Add personal savings coverage period
3. Set deferred period to match
4. Balance cost vs protection

**Example**:
- Employer sick pay: 3 months (13 weeks)
- Emergency fund: 3 months (13 weeks)
- Total coverage: 6 months (26 weeks)
- **Recommendation**: 26-week deferred period

### Benefit Period

**How long will policy pay**:
- **To retirement age**: Best protection (but expensive)
- **5 years**: Mid-range option
- **2 years**: Budget option
- **1 year**: Minimal protection

**Recommendation**: To retirement age if affordable, otherwise 5 years minimum

## Business Protection Needs

### Keyman Insurance

**Purpose**: Compensate business for loss of key person

**Calculation Methods**:

**1. Profit-Based**:
```
Key Person's Contribution to Profit × Recovery Period
```
Example: £100,000 profit contribution × 5 years = £500,000

**2. Cost-Based**:
```
Recruitment Cost + Training Cost + Lost Productivity
```
Example: £50,000 + £30,000 + £100,000 = £180,000

**3. Multiple of Salary**:
```
Annual Salary × Multiple (5-10×)
```
Example: £60,000 × 7 = £420,000

### Shareholder Protection

**Purpose**: Enable surviving shareholders to buy deceased's shares

**Calculation**:
```
Company Valuation × Deceased's Shareholding %
```

**Example**:
- Company value: £2,000,000
- Shareholding: 25%
- Sum assured: £500,000

**Structure**:
- Each shareholder insured
- Other shareholders are beneficiaries
- Cross-option agreement in place

### Partnership Protection

**Similar to shareholder protection but for partnerships**

- Each partner insured
- Other partners buy out deceased's share
- Partnership agreement essential

## Needs Analysis Interview Questions

### Financial Position
- What is your annual gross income?
- What are your monthly expenses?
- Do you have savings/emergency fund? How much?
- What debts do you have? (mortgage, loans, credit cards)
- Do you own property? Current value? Outstanding mortgage?

### Family Situation
- Are you married/in a partnership?
- Do you have children? Ages?
- Does partner work? Income?
- Any elderly dependents?
- Future financial commitments? (school fees, university, weddings)

### Employment
- Employed or self-employed?
- Employer sick pay? How long?
- Employer death in service benefit? How much?
- Group income protection?
- Pension provision?

### Existing Protection
- Any existing life insurance? Amount? Type? Term?
- Critical illness cover?
- Income protection?
- When taken out? Reviewed recently?

### Objectives
- What concerns you most? (Death, serious illness, unable to work)
- How long would you want income replaced?
- Would you want mortgage paid off?
- What standard of living for family?
- Any specific goals? (children's education, etc.)

## Common Needs Analysis Mistakes

### Over-Insurance
- More cover than affordable
- Unrealistic income replacement
- Not considering existing provision

### Under-Insurance
- Ignoring inflation
- Forgetting future costs
- Assuming partner will cope financially

### Ignoring Existing Cover
- Workplace death in service
- Existing policies
- Savings and investments
- Spouse's income

### Wrong Cover Type
- Recommending life when IP needed
- Level when decreasing would suit
- Critical illness without explaining limitations

## Documentation

**Always Document**:
- Calculation method used
- Assumptions made
- Why sum assured is suitable
- Why alternatives rejected
- Client's budget constraints

**File Note Example**:
> Calculated protection need using comprehensive method. Total needs £624,000. Less existing cover £100,000 and savings £25,000. Net need £499,000. Recommended £500,000 level term to age 65 to cover mortgage, income replacement for 15 years, and children's university costs. Client confirmed this meets objectives and is affordable at £45/month.

## Review Triggers

**Review protection needs when**:
- Birth of child
- Marriage/partnership
- Divorce/separation
- House move (mortgage increase)
- Salary increase
- New debts
- Business started
- Every 3-5 years minimum

## Summary

**Good needs analysis**:
- Uses systematic method
- Considers all needs (immediate, ongoing, future)
- Accounts for existing provision
- Documents assumptions
- Results in suitable, affordable cover
- Can be justified if questioned

**Remember**: The goal is adequate protection that's affordable and sustainable. Perfect coverage that's unaffordable helps nobody.
