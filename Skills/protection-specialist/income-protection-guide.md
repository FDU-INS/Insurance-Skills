# Income Protection Guide

## What is Income Protection?

**Purpose**: Replaces lost income if unable to work due to illness or injury

**Pays**: Regular monthly income until you return to work, recover, retire, or policy ends

**Not**: Life insurance (pays on death) or Critical Illness (pays on specific diagnosis)

## How Income Protection Works

**Typical Structure**:
1. Get ill/injured and unable to work
2. Wait for deferred period (e.g., 13 weeks)
3. Submit claim with medical evidence
4. Monthly payments start
5. Continue until return to work or benefit period ends

**Example**:
- Benefit: £2,000/month
- Deferred period: 26 weeks
- Benefit period: To age 65
- Falls ill: Off work 12 months
- Claim process:
  - Weeks 1-26: No payment (deferred period)
  - Weeks 27-52: Receive £2,000/month (£16,000 total)
  - Return to work: Payments stop

## Key Policy Features

### 1. Benefit Amount

**Maximum Coverage**: Usually 50-70% of gross income

**Why Not 100%?**
- Prevents over-insurance
- Benefits usually tax-free (so 60% gross ≈ net pay)
- Provides incentive to return to work
- Matches financial need (lower expenses when off work)

**Calculating Benefit**:
```
Gross Annual Income: £50,000
Maximum 60%: £30,000/year
Monthly benefit: £2,500/month
```

**Less** (if applicable):
- Employer sick pay (time-limited)
- Statutory sick pay (£116.75/week)
- Other income protection
- State benefits

**Example**:
- Gross income: £50,000 (£4,167/month)
- Max IP benefit (60%): £2,500/month
- Less employer sick pay (6 months): £0 (after deferred period)
- IP benefit: £2,500/month ✓

**For Self-Employed**:
- Based on last 3 years' accounts (average)
- Or last year (if more recent)
- Dividend and salary combined
- Profit before drawings

### 2. Deferred Period (Waiting Period)

**Definition**: How long you wait before benefits start

**Common Options**:
- **1 week**: Very expensive, rarely necessary
- **4 weeks**: Expensive, self-employed use
- **8 weeks**: Common for self-employed
- **13 weeks** (3 months): Popular balance
- **26 weeks** (6 months): Cost-effective, works with sick pay
- **52 weeks** (12 months): Cheapest, long-term protection only

**How to Choose**:
1. Check employer sick pay duration
2. Add emergency fund coverage
3. Set deferred period to match
4. Balance cost vs protection

**Example 1: Employee**
- Employer sick pay: 6 months full pay
- Emergency fund: 3 months
- Total coverage: 9 months
- **Recommended deferred period**: 26 weeks (6 months)
- Rationale: Sick pay covers 6 months, savings bridge gap if longer

**Example 2: Self-Employed**
- No employer sick pay
- Emergency fund: 2 months
- Total coverage: 2 months
- **Recommended deferred period**: 8-13 weeks
- Rationale: Minimal external support, need earlier protection

**Cost Impact**:
- 4-week deferred: 100% (baseline)
- 13-week deferred: 60% of 4-week cost
- 26-week deferred: 40% of 4-week cost
- 52-week deferred: 25% of 4-week cost

### 3. Benefit Period (Payment Duration)

**Options**:
- **To retirement age** (60, 65, or 70): Best protection
- **5 years**: Mid-range option
- **2 years**: Budget option
- **1 year**: Minimal protection

**Recommendation**: To retirement age if affordable

**Why**:
- Long-term illness most financially devastating
- Short-term covered by savings/sick pay
- Small premium difference (5yr vs retirement)

**Cost Impact**:
- 1 year: Cheapest (but limited value)
- 2 years: Cheap (but insufficient for serious illness)
- 5 years: Good value
- To age 65: Best value per year of protection

**Example**:
- Age 35, £2,000/month benefit, 13-week deferred
- 2-year benefit: £30/month (covers up to 2 years)
- 5-year benefit: £38/month (covers up to 5 years)
- To age 65: £45/month (covers up to 30 years)
- **Best value**: To age 65 (only £7/month more than 5-year for 25 more years protection)

### 4. Definition of Incapacity

**Critical Difference Between Providers**

#### Own Occupation (Best)

**Definition**: Unable to do your own job

**Example**:
- Occupation: Surgeon
- Injury: Hand injury preventing surgery
- Still able to: Teach, consult, admin work
- Own occupation policy: **Pays** (can't do surgery)
- Any occupation policy: **Doesn't pay** (can do other work)

**Who Needs It**:
- Professionals (doctors, dentists, lawyers)
- Skilled trades (electricians, plumbers, chefs)
- Manual workers with specific skills
- Anyone in specialized occupation

**Cost**: 20-40% more than "Any Occupation"

#### Suited Occupation (Middle Ground)

**Definition**: Unable to do your job or similar work you're suited for

**Example**:
- Occupation: Accountant
- Injury: Back injury
- Can't do: Office work
- Could do: Phone-based advice (seated)
- Suited occupation: **May not pay** (suited to similar work)

**Less Clear**: More disputes, interpretation needed

#### Any Occupation (Budget)

**Definition**: Unable to do any job

**Example**:
- Occupation: Office manager
- Injury: Moderate back pain
- Can't do: Heavy lifting, long standing
- Can do: Light office work, desk job
- Any occupation policy: **Doesn't pay** (can do some job)

**Who It Suits**:
- Budget constraints only
- Low-skilled work (easier to prove total incapacity)
- Very limited income

**Risk**: Much harder to claim, many disputes

#### Activities of Daily Living (ADL)

**Definition**: Unable to perform basic daily tasks

**Tasks** (typically 6):
1. Walking
2. Climbing stairs
3. Lifting/carrying
4. Bending/kneeling
5. Sitting
6. Manual dexterity

**Usually requires** inability to do 3+ ADLs

**Very Strict**: Only for severe disability

**Avoid**: Unless extremely budget-limited

### 5. Deferred Period vs Benefit Period Combinations

**Example Combinations**:

| Deferred | Benefit | Cost | Best For |
|----------|---------|------|----------|
| 4 weeks | 2 years | £££ | Self-employed, limited sick pay, short-term focus |
| 13 weeks | 5 years | ££ | Balance of cost and protection |
| 13 weeks | To age 65 | ££+ | Comprehensive protection, affordable |
| 26 weeks | To age 65 | ££ | Employees with good sick pay, cost-conscious |
| 52 weeks | To age 65 | £ | Long-term catastrophic protection only |

**Most Popular**: 13-week deferred, to age 65, own occupation

## Additional Policy Features

### Rehabilitation Benefit

**What It Is**: Additional payment for rehabilitation costs

**Coverage**:
- Physiotherapy
- Counseling
- Retraining for new career
- Home adaptations
- Return to work support

**Typical Limit**: £10,000-£25,000 over policy life

**Cost**: Usually included free or minimal cost

**Value**: Can accelerate return to work

### Proportionate Benefit (Partial Disability)

**What It Is**: Part payment if partially disabled

**Example**:
- Full benefit: £2,000/month
- Return to work part-time (50%)
- Proportionate benefit: £1,000/month (50% of full)

**Conditions**:
- Usually after receiving full benefit for 6-12 months
- Must be working reduced hours due to continued incapacity
- Income must be reduced

**Value**: Encourages gradual return to work

### Indexation (Inflation Protection)

**What It Is**: Benefit amount increases annually

**Options**:
- **RPI**: Retail Price Index
- **CPI**: Consumer Price Index
- **Fixed %**: 3% or 5% per year

**In Payment**: Increases continue when claiming

**Cost**: Premiums increase each year (usually RPI + 1-2%)

**When to Use**:
- Long terms (20+ years)
- Younger clients (30s-40s)
- Inflation concern

**When to Skip**:
- Short terms (< 10 years)
- Tight budget
- Near retirement

### Waiver of Premium

**What It Is**: Premiums paid while claiming

**How It Works**:
- After claiming for 6-12 months
- Insurer pays your premiums
- Policy continues at no cost
- Until return to work or policy ends

**Cost**: Usually included free

### Guaranteed Insurability Options

**What It Is**: Increase cover without medical evidence

**Triggers**:
- Salary increase (>10-20%)
- Marriage
- Birth of child
- Mortgage increase

**Limits**: Usually up to 50% increase, maximum 3-5 times

**Cost**: Included or small additional premium

**Value**: Protects future insurability

## Pricing Factors

**Age**: Biggest factor
- Age 30: £20-30/month per £1,000/month benefit
- Age 50: £50-80/month per £1,000/month benefit

**Occupation Class**:
- **Class 1** (Professional): Lowest premiums
- **Class 2** (Skilled non-manual): Low premiums
- **Class 3** (Skilled manual): Medium premiums
- **Class 4** (Unskilled/heavy manual): Highest premiums

**Smoker**: +30-50% premium

**Health**: Significant impact
- Loadings or exclusions common
- Mental health history: Heavy loading or decline

**Deferred Period**: Longer = cheaper
- 4 weeks: 100%
- 26 weeks: 40-50% of 4-week cost

**Definition**: Own occ = more expensive
- Own occupation: 100%
- Suited: 80-90%
- Any occupation: 60-70%

## Tax Treatment

**Premiums**:
- **Personal**: Not tax deductible
- **Business**: Corporation tax relief if employer pays

**Benefits**:
- **Personal policy**: Tax-free to recipient
- **Business policy**: Taxable as income

**Recommendation**: Personal policy for tax-free benefits

## Employer vs Personal IP

### Employer Group IP

**Pros**:
- Employer pays (no personal cost)
- No medical underwriting (often)
- Cover starts immediately

**Cons**:
- Lose cover if leave job
- Usually "any occupation" (strict)
- May have long deferred period (6-12 months)
- Benefit taxable

### Personal IP

**Pros**:
- Portable (keep if change jobs)
- Own occupation definition (better)
- Shorter deferred periods available
- Benefits tax-free
- Control over cover

**Cons**:
- Personal cost
- Medical underwriting required
- May be declined/loaded

**Recommendation**: Personal IP supplements or replaces employer scheme

## Income Protection vs ASU (Accident, Sickness, Unemployment)

### Accident, Sickness & Unemployment (ASU)

**Coverage**:
- Accident
- Sickness
- Unemployment (redundancy)

**Typical Features**:
- Short-term (12-24 months max)
- Fixed monthly benefit (often £1,000-£1,500)
- 30-60 day deferred period
- Budget pricing (£15-30/month)

**Limitations**:
- Short benefit period (inadequate for long-term illness)
- Strict unemployment conditions (not always pays)
- Limited benefit amount

**Use**: Mortgage payment protection (short-term)

### Income Protection (Full)

**Coverage**:
- Illness or injury
- Long-term (to retirement)
- Higher benefits (50-70% income)

**Better Than ASU**: But more expensive

**ASU Has Place**: Short-term mortgage protection for those who can't afford full IP

## Claims Process

**Step 1: Notify Insurer**
- As soon as incapacitated
- Within 30 days (usually)

**Step 2: Complete Claim Form**
- Personal details
- Nature of incapacity
- Treatment received
- GP details

**Step 3: Medical Evidence**
- GP report (insurer requests)
- Specialist reports if needed
- Attend medical assessment (if required)

**Step 4: Assessment**
- Insurer reviews evidence
- May request additional information
- Decides if claim meets policy definition

**Step 5: Claim Acceptance**
- First payment after deferred period
- Ongoing payments (monthly)
- Regular reviews (every 3-6 months)

**Step 6: Ongoing Claims Management**
- Update insurer on progress
- Attend medical reviews
- Report return to work (full or part-time)
- Rehabilitation support if offered

**Claim Decline Reasons**:
- Doesn't meet definition of incapacity
- Pre-existing condition not disclosed
- Non-disclosure of material facts
- Returns to work before deferred period ends
- Can do alternative work (if "any occupation")

## Mental Health Coverage

**Coverage**: Yes, but...

**Challenges**:
- Harder to prove incapacity objectively
- More frequent reviews
- May require specialist psychiatric assessment
- Some policies limit mental health claims (e.g., max 2 years)

**Best Providers**:
- No mental health limitations
- Treat same as physical illness
- Cover to full benefit period

**Poorer Providers**:
- Limit mental health claims (12-24 months)
- Stricter evidence requirements
- May exclude certain conditions

**Underwriting**:
- History of depression/anxiety: Loadings or exclusions common
- Serious mental health history: May decline
- Current treatment: Often postponed

## Suitability Assessment

**IP is Suitable When**:
- Employed or self-employed with income
- Dependents or financial commitments
- Limited employer sick pay or none
- Can afford premiums
- Age under 60 (becomes very expensive after)

**IP May NOT Be Suitable When**:
- Significant savings to cover long-term absence
- Comprehensive employer sick pay (1+ years full pay)
- Near retirement
- Can't afford (tight budget - prioritize life insurance)
- Health issues make it unaffordable/unavailable

**Better Alternative (if unsuitable)**:
- ASU (short-term mortgage protection)
- Critical illness (lump sum if serious diagnosis)
- Increased emergency savings
- Employer group IP (if available)

## Common Client Objections

**"It's too expensive"**
- Consider longer deferred period (26 or 52 weeks)
- Reduce benefit period (5 years instead of retirement)
- Review budget (often more affordable than expected)
- Compare to income loss (£50k/year for illness vs £50/month premium)

**"I won't claim"**
- Statistics: 1 in 7 people off work 5+ years before retirement
- Illness/injury unpredictable
- Most claims are for back problems, mental health, cancer - not exotic conditions

**"I have savings"**
- How long would they last?
- Replace savings or protect them?
- What if illness is long-term (5+ years)?

**"My employer pays sick pay"**
- For how long? (often 3-6 months only)
- What then?
- Would savings cover gap?

**"State benefits will cover me"**
- Universal Credit: £84.80/week (£368/month)
- PIP: £28.70-£184.30/week
- Not sufficient for most people's needs
- Complex application, delays

## Review Triggers

**Review IP when**:
- Salary increase (>20%)
- New job (different occupation class)
- Mortgage increase
- Dependents increase (children born)
- Employer sick pay changes
- Every 3-5 years minimum

## Tax Planning for Business Owners

### Executive IP (Relevant IP)

**Structure**:
- Company pays premiums (allowable expense)
- Benefit paid to individual (not company)
- Benefits tax-free
- No P11D charge

**Tax Benefits**:
- Corporation tax relief: 19-25%
- No NI on premiums: 13.8% saving
- Tax-free benefits
- Outside estate (IHT-efficient)

**Example**:
- Premium: £1,200/year
- Corporation tax relief: £300 (25%)
- NI saving: £166
- Net cost to company: £734
- Benefit to director: £30,000/year tax-free

## Red Flags (Poor Advice)

- ASU recommended when full IP needed
- Any occupation when own occupation appropriate
- Short benefit period (1-2 years) for younger clients
- No IP recommended for main earner with dependents
- Employer scheme assumed adequate without checking
- No consideration of mental health coverage

## Best Practice

**Good IP advice includes**:
- Clear income needs analysis
- Employer sick pay considered
- Emergency fund factored in
- Deferred period aligned with coverage
- Own occupation recommended (if appropriate)
- Long benefit period (to retirement)
- Definition quality checked
- Mental health coverage confirmed
- Tax treatment explained
- Regular review scheduled
