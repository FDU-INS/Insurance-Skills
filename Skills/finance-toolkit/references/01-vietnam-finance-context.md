## Budgeting Framework

### 50/30/20 Adapted for HCMC

**50% Needs:**
- Housing (rent + utilities): ideally ≤ 30% of gross income
- Transport: motorbike maintenance, Grab budget, parking
- Food staples: wet market/supermarket groceries
- BHYT contributions (employer deducts automatically)
- Loan minimum repayments (if any)

**30% Wants:**
- Dining out (HCMC has world-class street food; easy overspend)
- Cafes (café culture is significant — budget explicitly)
- Entertainment, hobbies, subscriptions
- Travel (domestic: weekend trips 2–5M; international: 15–40M)
- Clothing, personal care

**20% Savings/Investments:**
- Emergency fund top-up until 3–6 months reached
- Long-term investments (VN30 ETF, bonds, gold)
- Wedding/home/retirement goal funds
- Can increase to 30–35% once emergency fund is complete

### Tracking Tools
- **Money Lover** — best VND-native app; syncs with Vietnamese bank accounts; shared wallet feature for couples
- **Misa Money Keeper** — popular in Vietnam, good for expense categorization
- **Bank apps** — Techcombank (iConnect), VPBank (NEO), MBBank — have built-in spending analytics

### Common Budgeting Mistakes
1. **Forgetting "social tax"**: wedding invitations (thư mời đám cưới) arrive in clusters; a couple may spend 5–20M/month during peak wedding season
2. **Tết budget shock**: New Year gifting, travel to hometown, red envelopes — budget 1–3 months' "Wants" in January/February
3. **No "yours/mine/ours" structure**: merged spending without clear boundaries leads to conflict
4. **Ignoring lifestyle creep**: salary increases absorbed by upgraded rent/dining without increasing savings rate
5. **Underestimating parents' support**: many couples budget 0 for this, then face it unexpectedly

## Emergency Fund

### Target
- **3 months minimum** (base safety): cover job loss or medical emergency
- **6 months ideal**: especially if self-employed or variable income
- **Formula**: Monthly essential expenses × months target
  - Example: 30M/month expenses → 90M (3 months) to 180M (6 months) target

### Where to Keep It

| Account Type | Liquidity | Typical Rate | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard savings (thanh toán) | Instant ATM | 0.1–0.5% | Emergency cash portion (20%) |
| Flexible savings (Tiết kiệm linh hoạt) | 1–5 days | 3–6% | Main emergency fund (60%) |
| Short-term deposit 1–3 months | End of term | 4–7% | Stable portion (20%) |
| Fintech (Tikop, Finhay) | 1–3 business days | 5–7% | Alternative to bank deposits |

**Recommended split:** 20% instant-access + 80% flexible/short-term deposits

### Bank Recommendations
- **Techcombank F@st Savings**: competitive rates, easy app access
- **VPBank NEO**: higher deposit rates than Big 4, digital-first
- **ACB, MB Bank**: solid options with competitive flexible savings
- **Avoid:** keeping entire emergency fund in current account (zero yield)

### Short-term Savings Products
- **Tiết kiệm có kỳ hạn** (fixed-term deposit): 1–24 months, rates vary by term
- **Tiết kiệm linh hoạt**: earn interest but can withdraw anytime (lower rate than fixed)
- **Chứng chỉ tiền gửi (CD)**: bank-issued certificates, slightly higher yield
- **Fintech accumulative accounts**: Tikop's "hũ" model, Finhay's savings goals — user-friendly, 5–7% returns

## Debt & Credit in Vietnam

### CIC — Credit Information Center
- Vietnam's national credit bureau (Trung tâm Thông tin Tín dụng)
- Tracks: credit cards, personal loans, home loans, car loans, BNPL services
- Late payment → stays on record for years → impacts future mortgage approvals
- **Check your CIC score**: via CIC website or Ngân hàng Nhà nước portal (free, 1–2 requests/year)
- Partners should both check individually before applying for joint loans

### Types of Consumer Debt (Vietnam)
| Type | Typical Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Credit card (unsettled balance) | 24–36% p.a. | Highest risk; pay in full every month |
| Consumer/personal loan (bank) | 12–20% p.a. | Use only for genuine needs |
| Personal loan (fintech/app) | 20–40%+ p.a. | Dangerous; often predatory |
| BNPL (mua trước trả sau) | 0–15% | OK if disciplined; tracked by CIC |
| Home mortgage | 8–12% p.a. | Acceptable leverage for asset |
| Education loan | 8–10% p.a. | Acceptable investment in human capital |

### Debt Repayment Strategies
- **Avalanche method**: Pay minimums on all; throw extra at highest interest rate → saves most money mathematically
- **Snowball method**: Pay minimums on all; throw extra at smallest balance → builds psychological momentum

**When debt is acceptable:**
- Mortgage on property with clear title (sổ hồng/sổ đỏ)
- Education investment with clear ROI
- Business loan with solid cash flow plan

**When debt is dangerous:**
- Credit card balance rolled month-to-month (24–36% compounding)
- Personal loans for lifestyle purchases (phone upgrades, travel)
- Multiple app loans (vay tiêu dùng qua app)
- Total debt repayments > 40% of combined take-home pay

### Couple Debt Rules
- Full transparency: both partners must know all debts of the other before marriage
- Joint mortgage: both CIC scores matter; the weaker score can impact approval
- Strategy: if one partner has debt, both prioritize eliminating it before taking on joint mortgage
- Rule of thumb: combined debt-to-income ratio < 40% before considering home loan
